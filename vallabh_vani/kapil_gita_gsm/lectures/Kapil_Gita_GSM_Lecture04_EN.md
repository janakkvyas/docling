# 4. Mātrā: Attributes Consolidated into Substance; Exist and Subsist; the Pointer with Two Ends

*[00:10:08]*

Now first of all, *mātrā* are supposed to be the attribute of substance. But the theory, Sāṅkhya theory, metaphysics of Sāṅkhya, is a unique in the sense that normally attributes are considered residing in the substance — but for Nyāya. But for Sāṅkhya, substance is a consolidation of the attributes. For instance, heat is an attribute and fire is substance. But whenever you enhance the heat, it comes — it turns into, culminates into fire. Like that. So the attributes being consolidated becomes the substance.

And what are the attributes again? Attributes are not hundred percent subjective nor hundred percent objective. They are connecting link between the what is perceivable and what is perceiver. And I have described them as a sense data. Means sensation, sensor: between sensation and sensor there is a sense data which — which — which is produced in collaboration of both, subject and object. That's why it is called *mātrā*. For instance, whether form, colour, shape, size, smell — all such attributes which are considered to be the attributes of the substance, perceivable substance: from eyes we perceive shape, size, colour etc.; from ear we perceive the sound, whether articulated sounds or inarticulated sound, means *dhvani* or *varṇa*; or they smell, or touch feeling — any of these substance attributes are not hundred percent quality of the substance. Some of the realist thinker consider them as a independent substance, independent attributes of the substance, because they consider that attributes are produced in the substance. They have opposite theory. While Sāṅkhya says, no, no, substance is produced out of the consolidation of the all the attributes.

And that's why we can say now, nowadays — for instance, matter and energy: whether energy subsist in the matter and matter exist, or whether matter subsist in the energy and energy exist? That's the dilemma.

What is the different between exist — exist and subsist? Attribute does not exist independently; it subsists. It subsists means subordinately exist to some substance. For instance, relation. Relation cannot be exist without the referent and relatum: say, cause and effect; father, parents and child. Now the relation cannot exist without the referent and relatum, means parent and child, or cause and effect, or brother-brother, or two friends; or let there be relation of the greater and higher — A is greater than B, or B is smaller than A — all this sorts of relation. They do not exist in space; they subsist in the matter, which matter exist in this space. That's a popular concept.

Sāṅkhya considers *mātrā* exist in this space, between the object and subject, so-called object and subject. And object and subject — because *mātrā* is there, so the one end is — one end becomes the subject and the other end becomes the object. For instance, we have two end of this pointer. This is a pointer, the handle; *mātrā* is this. And handle and pointer exist as a two end of this point. So subject and object exist as a — subject exist as a perceiver of *mātrā*, and object exists as a perceiver of a perceivable object. And *mātrā* is consolidated here, and in between *mātrā* is not consolidated; it exist in this space. So we have a feeling that *mātrā* exist in substance. That's the main reason for misunderstanding, that *mātrā* exist in substance. No: substance and the — the substance and the perceiver both exist as a two end of the *mātrās*; on one end perceiver exist, on the other end ‹…›. So *mātrā* is a main stuff, main stuff. That's a Sāṅkhya theory.

# Brief Summary

**Content.** The speaker resumes the two tracks of self-awareness: Yoga proceeds by *ātma-viveka-khyāti*, isolating the self, while Sāṅkhya proceeds negatively by *anātma-viveka-khyāti*, enumerating what is not self; both remove the twin faults of non-comprehension of distinction and comprehension of non-distinction, described in Śrīmad Bhāgavata 2.9.33, and both arrive at the one destination, *svarūpāvasthāna*, as Bhagavad Gītā 5.4–5 says. Following Mahāprabhuji's kārikās on the twenty-fifth *adhyāya*, he explains why nine chapters treat a ninefold *prakṛti* and how *abhāva*, no further birth, follows when God takes form. The core of the session is the Sāṅkhya doctrine of *mātrā*: substance is a consolidation of attributes rather than their seat; *mātrā* exists, while perceiver and perceivable subsist in it as its two ends. This makes Sāṅkhya neither idealism nor realism but a synthesis that dissolves the European square of realism, idealism, rationalism and empiricism, and it is the metaphysics Mahāprabhuji adopted for the categories of Śuddhādvaita. The egocentric starting point is a necessary illusion of being the centre, to be diluted, not destroyed. Hari is the true enjoyer and primordial perceiver; individual consciousness is his sense organ, so that *bhakti-yoga* brings *vaitṛṣṇya* and the attitude of *nimitta-mātra*, with the partial initiative of reflexes under God's control. In answer to a question, he explains that Kapila joins *bhakti* to both Sāṅkhya and Yoga out of regard for Devahūti's soft heart.
