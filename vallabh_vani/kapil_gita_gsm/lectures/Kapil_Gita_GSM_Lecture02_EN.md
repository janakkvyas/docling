# 1. Kardama's Assurance: Why Sādhana If God Is to Be Born?

*[00:00:00]*

**Speaker:** Last time we discussed up to fourth *kārikā*. Bhāgavatam:

> धृतव्रतासि भद्रं ते दमेन नियमेन च ।  
> तपोद्रविणदानैश्च श्रद्धया चेश्वरं भज ॥  
> स त्वयाराधितः शुक्लो वितन्वन्मामकं यशः ।  
> छेत्ता ते हृदयग्रन्थिमौदर्यो ब्रह्मभावनः ॥  
>
> *dhṛta-vratāsi bhadraṁ te damena niyamena ca |*  
> *tapo-draviṇa-dānaiś ca śraddhayā ceśvaraṁ bhaja ||*  
> *sa tvayārādhitaḥ śuklo vitanvan māmakaṁ yaśaḥ |*  
> *chettā te hṛdaya-granthim audaryo brahma-bhāvanaḥ ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.24.3–4)
>
> [So Kardama Ṛṣi assures his wife Devahūti: then there will be incarnation of Viṣṇu from your womb, so don't get depressed.]

And in that context Mahāprabhujī explains in Subodhinī: *tarhi sādhanaṁ na kartavyam ity āha dhṛta-vratā iti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. If suppose God is going to be incarnated, take birth from me, what is the need of doing all the *sādhanas*, spiritual efforts? But there is a difference. How? *Dhṛta-vratā* means Devahūti has taken vow. So even if God will be incarnated through his body — through her body — she has to fulfil‹?› until the promise of taken vow, fulfilled vow.

*Yadā bhagavān svayam evāgamiṣyati* ‹श्रवणाधारित› — so Mahāprabhu says *sādhanāni siddhe bhagavati pravartante* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Tools, all these tools, spiritual efforts, are activated when there is God, because *sādhana* has some direction. And if they do not find the direction, divine direction, *sādhana* cannot take its place in person. But when Bhagavān, God himself, is about to come, then what is the use of *sādhana*, effort?

So Mahāprabhujī says: no, no, even then there is some role of *sādhana*. And how? *Sādhanāni kṛtāni bhagavantaṁ bodhayanti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. When you execute your spiritual efforts, those efforts inspire God. Not only inspire God, they reach God. And not only they reach God, but *vaśīkurvanti*: you become capable to take control of God.

*Yathā loke svabhāvato* — and there Mahāprabhu gives an example — *yathā loke svabhāvato bhoktāram atithiṁ nimantraṇādinā vaśīkṛtya bhojayanti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. As in the world, those who enjoy taking food — those who have a nature of taking food — they can be invited for dinner or lunch. Those who are fasting, how can we invite them? So by inviting them we pressurize them, because invitation pressurizes the person who enjoys eating, to come and take food at our place. Not the enjoyer — means by means of‹?› — but somebody who is fasting, he cannot be compelled. *Yato bhagavān svayam āgantā* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: because God is coming himself, *ataḥ sādhanāni kartavyāni*: therefore one has to perform the *sādhana*, ritual.

# 2. Why the Kapila Gītā Is in Our Syllabus

*[00:05:20]*

**Speaker:** Actually my purpose to incorporate in our syllabus this Kapila Gītā, particularly the two chapters, that is of Yoga and Sāṅkhya, was this: that Sāṅkhya chapter deals with all those categories which Mahāprabhujī has admitted in Sarvanirṇaya and explain as a doctrine of the Vallabha Vedānta. And in the Yoga chapter — it will come after certain pages — that Kapila Ṛṣi, Bhagavān Kapila, while teaching yoga to his mother, he mixed that principle of yoga with devotion. Why? Because Kapila, being son of Devahūti, knew very well how delicate nature of his mother is. So it will not be proper for him to inspire his mother to practise yoga. But then what he taught his mother was a mixture of devotion and yoga both.

# 3. Yājñavalkya and Maitreyī, Kapila and Devahūti

*[00:07:24]*

**Speaker:** Now one more problem here is: if we contrast this Devahūti statement with the Yājñavalkya–Maitreyī *saṁvād* in Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad. Yājñavalkya says, "Just tomorrow I am going to renounce the household life and become monk, practise monk‹?›. And because I am going to renounce this household life, so I have made a partition of my property between you and your co-wife Gārgī" — Maitreyī and Gārgī, somewhat like that…

**A student:** Gārgī?

**Speaker:** Kātyāyanī. Kātyāyanī and Maitreyī. "So that you should not have complaint against me that I have left both of you unsupported." And Maitreyī suddenly raises the question: "By renouncing us, what you want to get? Please tell me," she asked her husband. And Yājñavalkya says that, "I want to achieve liberation." So Maitreyī asked him, "The property, the portion of property, half portion of property that you are leaving for me, will it give me liberation?" Yājñavalkya said, *amṛtatvasya tu nāśāsti vittena* (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.2): "You cannot acquire liberation through the property." And then Maitreyī said, *yenāhaṁ nāmṛtā syāṁ kim ahaṁ tena kuryām* (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.3): "You are leaving us, you are renouncing us to take some higher reward. And if you consider me as your beloved, why don't you share that with me instead of sharing property with me?" So Yājñavalkya then preached for all the Upaniṣad *vidyā*. That was a dialogue between husband and wife, Maitreyī and Yājñavalkya.

Same episode here occurs between son and mother, son and mother. Kapila Ṛṣi was about to renounce the household life — though he was not married, but being son. He said to his mother that, "I am going tomorrow to renounce this world." And then Devahūti is confused: "Because I have given you birth, I have brought up you, and I have love for you as my son, and I knew that you are God, you are incarnation of God. But you think that you should renounce me. Then please tell me, why should I love you? Is my love for you an illusion, is a delusion, is in ignorance? Or my love for you has truly some praiseworthy, positive points?"

And then her son Kapila started teaching her yoga and Sāṅkhya. Because point of mother was that, "If you renounce me, that I should have renounced you before that, if renunciation is the true aim of life." Because in one of the Upaniṣad it is said, *na karmaṇā na prajayā dhanena tyāgenaike amṛtatvam ānaśuḥ* (Kaivalya Upaniṣad 1.3): neither by giving birth to child nor by acquiring property; by renouncing all this one becomes immortal. "So I should have practised that means of renunciation instead of bringing you up, making you this much bigger, that you are actually…" — not threatening in her word, but in a way it was threatening to her that, "Tomorrow I am renouncing this world." "Was my love a fake love, or true love? Was I am supposed to love you, or just neglect you? Please tell me what is my duty." So he asked for the *tyāga* and *vairāgya*: "Because you are taking *tyāga* and *vairāgya*, detachment and the renunciation, should I also follow you there, being your mother?" And Kapiladevjī was not considering it proper that his mother should follow him in the course of the detachment and the renunciation. So he taught her yoga and Sāṅkhya.

# 4. Why Sāṅkhya for Devahūti: The Four Āśramas

*[00:14:13]*

**Speaker:** And Mahāprabhujī clarified that why he taught Sāṅkhya. Because Devahūti was female, and in those days females were not supposed to renounce this world. Because *caturthāśrama* was strictly meant for Brāhmin only, not even for Kṣatriyas. Because in those days order was this: that Brāhmin has to go through all the four *āśramas*, *brahmacarya*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha* and *saṁnyāsa*; Kṣatriya has to go through the three *āśramas*, *brahmacarya*, *gṛhastha* and *vānaprastha*; Vaiśyas has to go through the two *āśramas*, *brahmacārī* and *gṛhastha*; and Śūdra was supposed to be one *āśrama*, *ekāśramī*, that is *gṛhasthāśrama*. This was their protocol of that time. So Devahūti was not supposed to follow; it was not fit for Devahūti to follow him the path of renunciation and detachment.

But Sāṅkhya — Mahāprabhujī says that's true. The Brāhmin has a… Brāhmin as a deep‹?› — not nowadays Brāhmin, in those days Brāhmin. Forget about this: ‹nowadays› Brāhmin does not have at all this type of inclination. In those days it was said, *brāhmaṇasya hi deho 'yaṁ kṣudra-kāmāya neṣyate* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 11.17.42): this body, physical body of Brāhmin, is not meant for the *kṣudra kāma* — means satisfying the householder's wants and all that — but it is meant… The purpose of the Brāhmin body is for *tapa*, *vairāgya* and liberation. So that was the inclination, innate inclination, of Brāhmaṇas on those days. Opposite is the case of nowadays Brāhmaṇa.

So Devahūti was not supposed to follow, because she was Brāhmin's *patnī*, not Brāhmin; and Brāhmin is supposed to follow the path of detachment and the renunciation, *caturthāśrama*. But being female she cannot follow the course of *caturthāśrama*, *saṁnyāsa*. That doesn't mean, Mahāprabhu says, that she does not aspire for the liberation. And liberation — anybody can aspire for liberation. So Sāṅkhya comes there, particularly this Kapila Sāṅkhya: those like *strī*, *śūdra*, or Kṣatriya or Vaiśya, all can aspire for liberation through the Sāṅkhya, but not through the *saṁnyāsa-mārga* of *caturthāśrama* of Veda. That was protocol. And for that reason Devahūti was taught by her son Sāṅkhya–Yoga. Because there is no prohibition in yoga and Sāṅkhya, that female should not do this or non-Brāhmin should not follow this; anybody can follow.

# 5. Yoga Mixed with Devotion: The Bouquet of Puṣṭimārga

*[00:18:13]*

**Speaker:** So Kapila Bhagavān preached this Sāṅkhya–Yoga. But while preaching Sāṅkhya–Yoga… I am not talking about Sāṅkhya, because Sāṅkhya is a philosophy, and yoga is to be practised based on that philosophy, the practical side of the Sāṅkhya. But he took little bit liberty, *suo motu* liberty, to teach his mother the admixture of yoga and devotion. Because he considered that his mother is soft person, and the yogic practices are little bit harsh for a female to practise — in those days, not nowadays. Nowadays ladies are joining army and all that, so there is no differentiation of harshness or softness. Today's protocol does not demand such things. This is the background.

And in this background he says, *yato bhagavān svayam āgantā, ataḥ sādhanāni kartavyāni*. Yes, what I was trying to tell you: that when Kapiladev teaches her the *bhakti*, mixing with, of course, yoga, those elements of the *bhakti* are incorporated by Śrī Mahāprabhujī also in his preaching of the Ṣoḍaśa Grantha, *bhakti*. So we can see here: if we can understand this yoga part of the preaching of Kapila, we can find the foundation of the Ṣoḍaśa Grantha here, particularly *sevā*, *śaraṇāgati*, all these, *samarpaṇa*. So many elements of our devotion are taken from Kapila Gītā as well as from the Uddhava Gītā, from the Bhāgavata. So the two Gītās played the major role of constructing the flower bouquet of Puṣṭimārga devotional.

# 6. Dhṛtavratā: Kardama, Not Kapila, Is Teaching Her

*[00:21:09]*

**Speaker:** So *ataḥ sādhanāni kartavyāni; tatra kānicid ‹deha›-śuddhy-artham ādau kartavyāni, tāni tava na kartavyānīty arthaḥ: dhṛta-vratāsi* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. So some exercise, spiritual exercise, must be performed for purification of body. But you need not require that, because you are already *dhṛta-vratā*, you have taken the vow. *Pātivratya-vrataṁ dhṛtam eva tvayā vartate* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: you have already taken vow of *pātivratya*. *Ataḥ paraṁ tvayi gate gamiṣyatīti cet, tatrāha* ‹श्रवणाधारित›.

So at present Kapila, Kapiladev, is not teaching her; Kardama Ṛṣi is teaching her. Please take it, do not confuse it with the teaching of the Kapiladev. Kapiladev teaching come from after the birth — the narration of the birth of the Kapila. Here Kardama is teaching, her husband. So *pātivratyaṁ dhṛtam eva tvayā vartate. Ataḥ paraṁ tvayi gate gamiṣyati*: if Kardama Gosvāmī renounces this world, then *pātivrata* will be forgotten. *Tan neti cet, tatra bhadraṁ ta iti; bhadram astu. Ātma-vākyād eva vrata-karaṇe 'pi vṛttir iva tava phalaṁ bhaviṣyati* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Husband assures her that even if you do not practise *pātivratya*, like all the *vṛtti*, you will get the reward of *pātivratya*.

# 7. The Five Sādhanas: Dama, Niyama, Tapas, Draviṇa and Dāna

*[00:23:15]*

**Speaker:** *Siddhe vrate bhagavat-prasādārthaṁ pañca sādhanāni kartavyānīty āha* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. And when the *pātivratya* is *siddha*, you have to perform five spiritual practices to acquire the grace of God. And out of that, *indriya-nigraha*, *dama*: *dama* means *indriya-nigraha*. *Niyama* means *bhagavadīyair eva dharmair vyavahartavyam* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: the rules that one has to practise, Bhāgavata dharma. *Niyamāḥ snānādayo vā dehasya*: or *niyama* could also mean the *snānādi*. *Tadā cakāreṇa prathamo grāhyaḥ* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. So *indriya-nigraha* has a prime importance.

*Tapaḥ prasiddhaṁ ‹kṛcchrādi›-rūpam* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Now *tapaḥ* here means — because it has *tapo-draviṇa-dānaiś ca*, *damena niyamena ca*, *tapo* — so what does Kardama Ṛṣi means by *tapa*? It is *‹kṛcchrādi›-rūpa*, means fasting and all that. *Draviṇāni dravyāṇi suvarṇādīni bhagavad-arthaṁ kartavyāni* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: and *draviṇa* means gold, money, etcetera; so those should be used for the sake of God. *Dravya-dānaṁ cānyat. Dānam eva anyathā avāntara-bahutve bahuvacanaṁ vyarthaṁ syāt* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. So here it is used in the plural sense, *tapo-draviṇa-dānaiś ca*, not *dānena*. So the plural sense implicates the usage of the money for God, and donation also.

*‹Prayatātmana› iti vākyāt; damo niyata-vratānāṁ hari-toṣa-janakatvād āvirbhāve 'pi te 'pi prayojakāḥ* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. *‹Prayatātmana›* means with wholehearted effort. So *iti vākyāt, damo niyataḥ*: *dama* means control of the senses is compulsory; and *vratānām*, the vows that taken; *hari-toṣa-janakatvāt*, because they please the Hari. So even after the incarnation — even for the incarnation — they play the major role.

*Yajño dānaṁ tapaś caiveti bhagavad-ukta-śāstrārtha-karaṇe bhagavān parituṣyatīti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. So in Gītā Bhagavān says, *yajño dānaṁ tapaś caiva pāvanāni manīṣiṇām* (Bhagavad Gītā 18.5), *etāny api tu karmāṇi* (Bhagavad Gītā 18.6), *saṅgaṁ tyaktvā karoti yaḥ, lipyate na sa pāpena padma-patram ivāmbhasā* (Bhagavad Gītā 5.10): *yajña*, *tapa* and *dāna*, these three, are most desirable course of the religious conduct. So if we act accordingly, we please God.

*‹Draviṇa›-padena ca yajño bhagavān ‹makha›-rūpa uktaḥ* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. So how to utilize money for God? So he said, in the form of *yajña*: *‹makha›-rūpa uktaḥ*. *Śraddhātra sarvāṅgam*: and the *śraddhā* plays the assisting role for all these spiritual efforts.

# 8. Īśvaraṁ Bhaja: Sādhana as a Fruitless Obligation

*[00:27:45]*

**Speaker:** *Nanu siddhe kim etāvanti sādhanānīty āśaṅkya tatrāha īśvaram iti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. But if God is suppose going to incarnate through me, through my womb, why that many, many efforts? So he says — Kardama Ṛṣi says — *īśvaraṁ bhaja*. *Īśvaraḥ sa na kenāpi niyamyaḥ; ato 'siddhavad eva sādhanāni kartavyānīty arthaḥ* ‹श्रवणाधारित›.

See, as I was telling you, many, many elements of Puṣṭimārga have been taken from here. So if you want, you can put the underline: *ato 'siddhavad eva sādhanāni kartavyānīty arthaḥ*. In this our course, number of students ask me: if this *kartavya* is *maryādā-mārgī*, then we are supposed to follow them or not? If we are Puṣṭimārgī, then why we should follow *maryādā-mārgī*'s exercise? But what Mahāprabhujī says: *īśvaraṁ bhaja*. It is a part of devotion that you perform all your duties, not considering duties that duty will give you — duty will enable you to achieve God. God will be achievable only by his own sweet will. So then why should I do effort, spiritual effort? So you should do spiritual effort. You should go for spiritual effort considering them as a fruitless but obligatory — a fruitless obligation.

# 9. Varṇāśrama-abhimāna: The Man on the Footpath and the Left Side of the Road

*[00:30:16]*

**Speaker:** And that's why many time, when people ask me, "Should we follow this, or should we follow that, or not?", I always give a standard answer: if you have a *varṇāśrama-abhimāna*, then you follow; if you do not have *varṇāśrama-abhimāna*, then you are not supposed to follow. We cannot say both.

For instance, if you are walking, you cannot put your leg on somebody sleeping on footpath and cannot give the excuse, "I did not see, I could not see him." No: while walking you are supposed to see also; you cannot blindly walk. But suppose if it is not your mistake, and you put the leg accidentally, or somebody comes suddenly and lie before you and you have put your leg upon him, then it is not your fault. Like that we have to take care: if we have a *svābhimāna*, *varṇāśrama-abhimāna*, to follow our duty. If we do not have *varṇāśrama-abhimāna*, you need not follow.

For instance — I will give you, simplify this issue — if you are driving a car in India, you have to follow the RTO rule of the India, to drive car on left side. But if you are not in India, you are in USA, and you can drive car in the right side also. There you cannot say that, "Because I am Indian, so I will drive my car on left side only, and no American will be allowed here to drive his car right side only because he is American." So where you are in some surrounding, you have to follow the rules and regulations of the surrounding. Like that, if you have a *abhimāna* of *varṇa* and *āśrama*, then you have to follow. Suppose if you do not have a *varṇāśrama-abhimāna*, it's not compulsory on you. You cannot say both, that, "I have a *varṇa-abhimāna*, *āśrama-abhimāna*, but I will follow only devotion."

So that line: *yāvad dehābhimānas tāvad varṇāśrama-dharma eva svadharmaḥ, bhagavad-dharmādayo 'pi vidharmāḥ para-dharmā vā. Yadā punaḥ saṅghāta-vyatiriktam ātmānaṁ manyate, tadā bhagavad-dharma eva svadharmaḥ, varṇāśramādayo 'pi para-dharmā vidharmā vā* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Till you have a self-awareness of *varṇāśrama*, your primary duty is to follow *varṇāśrama*, fulfil *varṇāśrama* duty. If you have somehow your *abhimāna* is shattered, that you belong to particular this caste or this stage of life, then *sevā* is the only your duty, and *varṇāśrama* duty becomes either no duty for you, or duty of the others, not of yours. That is the judgment given by Mahāprabhujī, and in Nibandha. But you can see here it stresses: *asiddhavat kartavyāni*.

# 10. Sa Tvayārādhitaḥ Śuklaḥ: The Son Who Will Cut the Knot of the Heart

*[00:34:23]*

**Speaker:** *Tataḥ kiṁ bhaviṣyatīty āśaṅkya sa tvayeti; mayārādhito 'pi tvayā ced evam ārādhitaḥ* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. I have pleased God, but if you also please God, then *sa hi śuklaḥ, nirdoṣa-pūrṇa-guṇa-vigrahaḥ*: that God who is *nirdoṣa-pūrṇa-guṇa-vigraha*, whose body is full of the good qualities, devoid of any blame. *Loke kardamasya putro jāta iti kīrtiṁ vitanvan* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: then people will sing the glory of mine also, that Kardama had a son, God, as an incarnated God. *Hṛdaya-granthiṁ bhetsyati*: and that son will unlock your heart. *Avaśya-kartṛtve‹?› viśvāsārthaṁ ca audarya iti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: and the incarnation of God is there in your womb.

*Nanu tasya mat-putrasya kathaṁ mad-ajñāna-dūrīkaraṇa-sāmarthyam* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: and don't doubt that, if he is taking birth as my son, how will he be able to remove my ignorance. Because *brahma bhāvayati anubhāvayati svasminn anyasmiṁś ca* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: this incarnation will be such that it will give the expression, Brahmic expression, within himself and for others too. ‹…› [a Sanskrit sentence here was rendered into English by the recogniser: "Therefore you also, Brahma, appearing in the heart's gland, having pierced it, gave this assurance"; the Sanskrit is not recoverable] So therefore you will also have, after giving birth to such incarnation of God, your *hṛdaya-granthi*, the knots of ignorance in your heart, will be unlocked, dissolve.

# 11. Devahūti's Śraddhā and the Birth of Kapila

*[00:36:52]*

**Speaker:**

> देवहूत्यपि संदेशं गौरवेण प्रजापतेः ।  
> सम्यक् श्रद्धाय पुरुषं कूटस्थमभजद्गुरुम् ॥  
>
> *devahūty api sandeśaṁ gauraveṇa prajāpateḥ |*

# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session continues the Subodhinī on Kardama's words to Devahūti before the birth of Kapila. If God himself is to be born of her, why should she perform *sādhana*? Mahāprabhujī answers that *sādhanas* reach God and bring him under control, as an invitation obliges a guest who enjoys food, so they are still to be done. The speaker then explains why the Kapila Gītā is in the syllabus: its Sāṅkhya chapter gives the categories Mahāprabhujī admits in the Sarvanirṇaya, and its yoga chapter mixes yoga with devotion, which is a foundation of the Ṣoḍaśa Grantha. He sets Devahūti's question against Maitreyī's question to Yājñavalkya: if renunciation is the aim, was her love for her son a delusion? Sāṅkhya was taught to her because *saṁnyāsa* was closed to women and non-Brāhmins while liberation through Sāṅkhya was open to all. Returning to the text, he reads the five *sādhanas* (*dama*, *niyama*, *tapas*, *draviṇa*, *dāna*) with *śraddhā* assisting all of them, and *īśvaraṁ bhaja* as the rule that *sādhanas* are done *asiddhavat*, as a fruitless but obligatory duty. From the Nibandha he draws the rule that *varṇāśrama* duty binds as long as one has *varṇāśrama-abhimāna*, and *sevā* alone once that *abhimāna* is gone. The verses on Kapila's birth lead to the two kinds of divine form: the forms known to the Veda and the forms that please the devotees, *lokaveda-prasiddha* and *lokavedātīta*, are both forms of one Puruṣottama, not two persons. God's human form is form, not flesh, and the Veda negates only a fixed number of forms, not the forms themselves.
