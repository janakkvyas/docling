# 1. Pradhāna: the Three Guṇas from Saccidānanda

*[00:00:00]*

**Speaker:** Today we have to learn Subodhinī of the tenth kārikā of Bhāgavata, which describe — which defines the *pradhāna prakṛti*.

> श्रीभगवानुवाच  
> यत्तत्त्रिगुणमव्यक्तं नित्यं सदसदात्मकम् ।  
> प्रधानं प्रकृतिं प्राहुरविशेषं विशेषवत् ॥  
>
> *śrībhagavān uvāca*  
> *yat tat triguṇam avyaktaṁ nityaṁ sadasadātmakam |*  
> *pradhānaṁ prakṛtiṁ prāhur aviśeṣaṁ viśeṣavat ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.10)
>
> [That which is of three qualities, unmanifested, eternal, both cause and effect, they call the *pradhāna*, the *prakṛti*: devoid of particularity, and possessing all the particulars.]

*Yat tad iti yat pūrvam uktaṁ tad eva etad etādṛg-guṇa-viśiṣṭam*‹श्रवणाधारित›. As it is said before, that *puruṣa*, *prakṛti*, *etādṛśa-guṇa-viśiṣṭa* — such, with such qualities, is considered as the primordial nature. *Yat triguṇam*: and that nature is consisted of three qualities. *Sā parā prakṛtiḥ, sā prakṛtiḥ sattva-rajas-tamo-guṇā*‹श्रवणाधारित›: it has three attributes, three qualities, *sattva*, *rajas*, *tamoguṇa*. *Yathā saccidānanda-rūpaṁ brahma, kriyā-jñānānanda-dharmā api bhavanti*‹श्रवणाधारित›. As Brahman is considered *saccidānanda*, so corresponding to *saccidānanda* Brahma: when *ānanda* concealed itself — *ānanda* attribute concealed itself — it becomes *tamas*. And *cit* attribute cancelled‹?› itself — when *ānanda* — not concealed itself, sorry, but when *ānanda* attribute of *saccidānanda* Brahman modifies into the *sad-aṁśa*‹?›, it becomes *tamoguṇa*. *Cid-aṁśa*, the attribute of Parabrahman, modifies into the *sad-aṁśa*, it becomes *rajoguṇa*. And the *sad-aṁśa*, when modified into the *sad-aṁśa*‹?›, it becomes *sattvaguṇa*. *Kriyā-jñānānanda-dharmā api bhavanti*: and therefore *kriyā*, the action or motion — existence acquires the motion, character of motion; and the consciousness acquires the character of knowledge; and *ananta*, infinity, acquires the character of *ānanda*, in the form of attribute. *Saccidānanda-rūpaṁ pradhānam*: so *saccidānanda* form is the primordial form. *Udgatās tu aṁśato guṇā api bhavanti*‹श्रवणाधारित›: when they manifest, they partially become attribute as well as substance.

And that remains *avyakta*. *Avyakta* means non-manifested — to us, not to Brahman. Not by any way expressed; the work itself is expressed. The effect of such nature is manifested to us, not self. *Nityam*: it is eternal. Since it is eternal, so therefore it, by itself, it doesn't go to the any transformation; it remains always same. *Sadasadātmakam*: *sadasadātmakam* means it is manifested as well as unmanifested, cause, and in the form of cause and effect. *Pradhānaṁ mukhyaṁ, prakṛtim iti lakṣyam*‹श्रवणाधारित›: so *prakṛti* is *lakṣya*, definable, and *pradhāna* is the main definition. *Prāhur iti pramāṇam*: when it is said, it is said — that is, refer — the saying refer to the *pramāṇa*. *Prakṛtir api brahmavad aviśeṣā*‹?›: means it does not have any particularity, because all the particularities are created or generated by the qualities of the *pradhāna*. *Ata eva viśeṣavat*: therefore, being devoid of any particularity, it creates varieties of the particular. *Pradhānam iti lakṣaṇam ekam eva*: therefore the basic *lakṣaṇa* is *pradhāna*.

Why it is called *pradhāna*, main? *Iti mukhyaṁ bhagavatā jagat-kāraṇatvena nirmitaṁ tad-rūpaṁ vā*‹श्रवणाधारित›: because God has taken this form as a substance of the world. *Tasya jagat-kartṛtva-nirvāhārthaṁ bhagavata iva ṣaḍ-guṇāḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›: and this *pradhāna* also becomes *jagatkartā*. Now, we have seen Brahman is *jagatkartā*; then question may be raised, why *pradhāna* is again *jagatkartā*? But Brahman is *jagatkartā* in the sense that it creates the *pradhāna*, and *jagatkartā* is the creator in the sense that all the material substances are produced by this *prakṛti*. And since this *prakṛti* produces material substance of the universe and it remains *avyakta*, unmanifested, so therefore many of the thinkers consider that world is not created by the creator, but world is an automatic creation. As it is said by Jains and Buddhas, that world does not require any creator; it is suo motu creative factor, force. But Vedānta would say, yes, it is due to the creation of Brahman within itself, as the material cause of the universe. So they — causality and creativity, both are the attributes of *prakṛti*. But it is *avāntara*. Why it is *avāntara*? Because the main cause and main creative creator is Brahman. But it is a — it is a creator and cause intermediary.

# 2. The Six Qualities of Prakṛti, Parallel to the Six of Bhagavān

*[00:08:22]*

**Speaker:** So as Bhagavān has six qualities, so *prakṛti* also has six qualities, and those six qualities are being described here. *Triguṇādi-ṣaṭ-padaiḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›. *Trividhā hi sṛṣṭir anyathā na bhavet*‹श्रवणाधारित›. First of all it is *triguṇātmikā*. Because it is *triguṇātmikā*, therefore creation also has a quality of triple quality, of — it is being produced, it is being sustained and it is being destroyed. *Yathā bhagavata aiśvaryaṁ, tathā; samāne karmaṇi ucca-nīca-gatayo na syuḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›. As it is — and this *triguṇatva* is corresponding to the *aiśvarya* of Brahman. Otherwise, if such a God is not there, then *samāne karmaṇi ucca-nīcatvaṁ*, *ucca-nīca-gatayo na syuḥ*. Same action is performed: through the *sāttvika* aspect it gives you different result, through the *rajas* aspect it gives you different result, and through the *tamas* aspect it gives you the third type of result. So that is *ucca-nīca-gati*.

*Mūla-dharmās te saccidānanda-ābhāsā bhagavata utpannāyāṁ pratiṣṭhitāḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›. These are the basic qualities, and they are pseudo-*saccidānanda*, and they are — they subsist in the nature which is produced by the God. Thus, truth alone is the main thing‹?›. So therefore, it is — since it is produced by God, therefore it is considered as main. *Yadyapi ekam eva lakṣaṇaṁ, tathāpi bhagavattāya ṣaḍ ucyante*‹श्रवणाधारित›. So one — one definition ‹…›. Notice — though it has a one definition, but just to draw the parallelism between Brahman, Bhagavān and *prakṛti*, the one definition is supplied with eight — six attributes.

Otherwise, with time, the expressed impermanence would not recreate itself‹?›. Otherwise, if nature would not have been there as a main cause of the universe, then what could be the other reason? *Kāla*, *karma* or *svabhāva*. So *kāla*, *karma*, *svabhāva* would be — if they play the role of cause in the creation, then this creation cannot be *nitya*. And once it is destroyed, it will be destroyed. But because it is *prakṛti*, so it's a constantly productive process, so it goes on creating and destroying.

# 3. Nityam, and the Two Exclusions of an Adjective: the Red Rose

*[00:12:24]*

**Speaker:** *Nityaṁ sadaika-rūpam iti kīrtiḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›. Now, when it is *nityam* said, *pradhāna*, in the kārikā — *yat tat triguṇam avyaktaṁ nityam* — so *nityam* means *sadā eka-rūpam iti*. This is *yaśoguṇa*, parallel to *yaśoguṇa*. *Tac ca jīva-prakṛti-vyudāsārtham iti kecid āhuḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›: *jīva-prakṛti-vyudāsārtham*, and some commentator consider this *nityam* to exclude the nature of the *jīva*. But Mahāprabhuji does not agree with this interpretation, because *tad uttarādhyāyena virudhyate*: this is contradictory to the coming chapter. *Nityatvād ubhayoḥ*: because *prakṛti* as *prakṛti* is *nitya*, so *puruṣa* is also *nitya*. So even if you consider *prakṛti* *nitya*, the *jīva*, *puruṣa* — that does not prove that *puruṣa* is *anitya*. Clear?

It must be taken into notice that *viśeṣaṇa* plays two types of role: *ayoga-vyavaccheda* and *anyayoga-vyavaccheda*. Like *eva-kāra*: *eva-kāra* has a three aspect, *anyayoga-vyavaccheda*, *ayoga-vyavaccheda* and *atyantāyoga-vyavaccheda*. But *viśeṣaṇa* also is not *atyantāyoga-vyavaccheda*, but *ayoga-vyavaccheda* and *anyayoga-vyavaccheda* is there. For instance, when we say "red rose", so it excludes the white rose, pink rose, blue rose et cetera: *anyayoga-vyavaccheda*. And sometime we use the adjective to *ayoga-vyavaccheda* — or *ayoga-vyavaccheda* means if somebody may doubt, "Can there be a red rose?" And when we say, "Yes, roses are red", in that sense it is *ayoga-vyavaccheda*. So *tad uttarādhyāyena, nityatvād ubhayor iti*.

*Sadasadātmakam iti*: it is both cause and it is also effect. Bhautika forms are of *prakṛti*. And that characteristic of *prakṛti* is — it is *śrī* — *śrīguṇa*, parallel to *śrīguṇa*. *Śrī* means *śobhā*. *Tasyā eṣā śobhā yat sarvātmakam*‹श्रवणाधारित›: because it takes all the form.

# 4. Aviśeṣam Viśeṣavat: the Black Deer and the Black Thermos

*[00:16:05]*

**Speaker:** *Aviśeṣam iti jñāna-hetuḥ; anyathā saṁsāriṇo muktā na syuḥ*‹श्रवणाधारित›. *Prakṛti* is devoid of particularity. Would there not have been particular — particularity in the nature, then there would be several natures; because particular — when we use the term particular, we use the term particular when there are number of instances of such phenomena. But the *prakṛti*, nature, is one. So since nature is one, so it is devoid of particularity. It's a unique concept.

Because universal characteristic and particular characteristic, both are relative term. For instance, this particular deer is black deer. So this — this deer: when we say "this particular is black deer", it's a universal characteristic; so particular characteristic of being this deer. Or say, this thermos is a black thermos. Now, this thermos is a particular, and a black thermos is a universal, because there can be several black thermoses; that's a universal characteristic of it. So both of the particularity and the universal characteristic, both are relative to each other. So in that case, if *prakṛti* would have been the particular, then there should be some universal characteristic of particular *prakṛti*, and then there would be numbers of *prakṛti* instead of one *prakṛti*. But there is only single *prakṛti*. So any item which is single cannot be considered as a particular. A single item among the universal is considered particular. For instance, a sole person cannot be considered as particular person, because if person is sole, one, singular — but numbers of persons are there; among them one person is a particular person. So since there are not many *prakṛtis*, so a single *prakṛti* cannot be considered as a particular *prakṛti*.

Now this *aviśeṣam* is *jñāna-hetu*. *Anyathā saṁsāriṇo muktā na syuḥ*: because if the *saṁsārī*, the *jīvas* which are in bondage, want to give up *prakṛti*, and if this were particular *prakṛti*, then if this particular were given — left, other *prakṛti* will chase him. So there will be impossibility of a *jīva* to get liberated from the *prakṛti* if numerous *prakṛtis* were there. But because it is one *prakṛti*, so in totality it can be given up. *Tasya sarva-kāraṇatvād viśeṣavat*‹श्रवणाधारित›: but because it is a cause of all the particulars, so it is *aviśeṣaṁ viśeṣavat* — devoid of particularity, and creator and possessing all the particulars. *Sarve viśeṣās tadīyā, na tasya kiñcid āścarya-karam iti vairāgyam*‹श्रवणाधारित›: all the particulars are the particulars of a phenomena which is not particular. And this is parallel to the *vairāgya*. So like Brahman, *prakṛti* also have six qualities.

# 7. The Tanmātras: Śabda Means Sound, and Pañcīkaraṇa

# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session reads Mahāprabhuji's Subodhinī on Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.10–18. The *pradhāna* or *prakṛti* is defined as of three *guṇas*, unmanifest, eternal, cause-and-effect, devoid of particularity yet possessing all particulars; its three *guṇas* are derived from the three aspects of *saccidānanda* Brahman, and its six qualities are read as parallel to the six of Bhagavān (*aiśvarya*, *yaśas*, *śrī*, *jñāna* and *vairāgya* are named). *Prakṛti* is an intermediary (*avāntara*) creator under Brahman, against the Jaina and Bauddha view of a self-creating world; Mahāprabhuji rejects the reading of *nityam* as excluding the *jīva*, since both *prakṛti* and *puruṣa* are eternal. The twenty-four elements are enumerated — five *mahābhūtas*, five *tanmātras*, ten *indriyas* and the fourfold *antaḥkaraṇa*, the four being named first as the movers — with digressions on direction as space in ten dimensions, *śabda* as sound rather than word, *pañcīkaraṇa*, and the definition of definition. Time is not a twenty-fifth substance of the *saguṇa* Brahman: on one view it is the influence of *puruṣa* on *prakṛti*, frightening only the one who acts under *ahaṅkāra*; on the Bhāgavata's own view it is the *ceṣṭā*, the first stirring of the *guṇas* from equilibrium, and Bhagavān is only indicated (*upalakṣita*) as *kāla*, being within all as *puruṣa* and without as time. Students' questions lead to an extended treatment of time — Big Bang, Spinoza, Kant, Śaṅkarācārya and Nyāya; being, becoming and subsistence; why "who created God?" cannot be asked of what is beyond time, Bhagavān as *kālasya kāla* — and to the relation of the *guṇas* to time through the transformation of being into becoming. The session stops before *mahat*.

**Examples and Stories.** The red rose and "roses are red" for the two exclusions of an adjective; the black deer and the black thermos for particular and universal; the sole person who cannot be a particular person; the *jīva* chased by another *prakṛti* if there were many; the whiteboard and the four corners for ten dimensions, the flat compass, and "news" as north, east, west and south; the musical notes *sa re ga ma* within *śabda*; the half-and-quarter mixture of *pañcīkaraṇa* and the impossibility of absolute vacuum; the still and the rippled pond for the *guṇas* disturbed; counting one's money when afraid of expenditure; the boyhood question about a car's mileage to the Kishangarh ruler's driver, "the habit of baniyās, not the rājās", and the same driver in a baniyā's service after the privy purses stopped; the Big Bang; the stopwatch; the recent book denying time; hibernation; the freeze frame of Satyajit Ray, and the closing of his film of the wife with the binoculars; the cameraman shooting creation and freezing its end; the relation of speaker and listener among eighteen participants; the escalator between two floors of an airport and the ladder given up on reaching the floor; infrared and ultraviolet; surrealism; the plane passing overhead; eyes closing in joy, in eating and in sex; the great-grandson's reminder of the day of his Brahmasambandha and the big belly.
