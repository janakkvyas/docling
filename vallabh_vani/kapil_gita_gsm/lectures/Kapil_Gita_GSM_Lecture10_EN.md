# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session opens with students' questions on the *citta*. *Mahat* is a synonym of Brahman: it is Brahman's caused form, which acts in its name like a general power of attorney. *Kūṭastha* (immobile) is relative, since cause and effect are relative terms. Taken cosmically the principle is *mahat*, taken individually it is *citta*, a good conductor of consciousness that stays awake in sleep. After death the soul stays near the body as *mṛta* until the body is destroyed and it becomes *preta*, and all four *antaḥkaraṇas* go with it. In Brahman *sat*, *cit* and *ānanda* are homogeneous. When they split, bliss is concealed in consciousness and consciousness in being, so matter is being with concealed consciousness, as in hair and nails. The speaker separates being from existence: time and space have being but do not exist in time and space, and consciousness likewise has being without existence. It knows *prakṛti* only through *prakṛti*'s own apparatus. Absolute peace (*ekāntika śāntatā*) differs from relative peace as absolute truth differs from truth, and the *citta* is purified the way water is: what does not belong to it is removed, and it returns to its own form. The text then moves from *citta* to the threefold *ahaṅkāra* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.24–26). The *sāttvika* gives rise to *man*, the *rājasa* to the *indriyas* and the *tāmasa* to the *bhūtas* and *tanmātras*. The divine form of *ahaṅkāra* is Saṅkarṣaṇa, one of the four *vyūhas* of the tantric account of creation (Vāsudeva as *citta*, Saṅkarṣaṇa as ego, Pradyumna as intellect, Aniruddha as mind). He sets the tantric realism, in which the mind creates a world that is real, against Śaṅkara's idealism, and places Sāṅkhya, which starts from *prakṛti* and *puruṣa*, between Tantra and Vedānta, where Brahman takes both forms. Consciousness sleeps on *ahaṅkāra* as Vāsudeva sleeps on Śeṣa, which is why a call wakes us. A student's question draws out the epistemological chain from sense to mind (alternatives) to *ahaṅkāra* to *buddhi* (decision) to memory, with the ego taking the credit at each step. *Ananta* means time, Saṅkarṣaṇa and Śeṣa. The suffix *mayaṭ* in *bhūtendriya-manomaya* carries both product and abundance. *Ahaṅkāra*'s marks are *kartṛtva* (*sāttvika*), *karaṇatva* (*rājasa*, since a cause must be able to change) and *kāryatva* (*tāmasa*), or else *śānta*, *ghora* and *vimūḍha*. Functions can become voluntary once self-awareness touches them. The dedicated person, as distinct from the devotee, gives up *ahaṅkāra*; what remains is like asymptomatic corona, deactivated but not removed.
