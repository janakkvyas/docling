# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session opens with a student's question whether illusion too has *ādhibhautika, ādhyātmika* and *ādhidaivika* forms. The speaker answers that it has: illusion about material things, the confusion of the self with the body-aggregate, and illusion about the Brāhmic, Paramātmic or Bhagavān nature of Kṛṣṇa, which Mahāprabhuji does not call illusion because it is sustained by *yogamāyā* for the sake of *bhakti* rather than by deluding *vyāmohikā māyā*. Since Kṛṣṇa is present at every place and time, he reciprocates whatever form the devotee thinks of (Bhāgavata 3.9.11; Gītā 9.29), so a devotional illusion becomes valid knowledge, as the face of Wittgenstein's cube follows the observer's attention. Answering a question about people who hear voices, he separates *pramāṇa-bala*, under which an attitude to God can be a delusion, from *prameya-bala*, under which the plastic object, God, makes the devotee's illusion real, and he likens this to the daydreams of inventors and artists. Taking up the five functions of *buddhi* (Bhāgavata 3.26.30), he explains *smṛti* as retention, distinct from recollection, recognition and remembering; shows from the Āyurvedic sevenfold classification and Freud's account of censorship and symbols that dream is not intensified memory, against Buddhist and Śaṅkara's views; defines deep sleep as the non-function of the intellect; and contrasts Śaṅkara's parallel *pramāṇa-vṛtti* and *avidyā-vṛtti*, with a witness consciousness, with Mahāprabhuji's view that ignorance, installed by God, can be removed only by God. Moving to the senses (3.26.31), he presents the *indriyas* as a *rājasa* buffer state between consciousness and matter, half of knowledge being the object, a position that denies both rationalism and empiricism. Causal necessity is a habit of the intellect, as Russell's *Nightmares of Eminent Persons* show; since creation is *līlā*, there is only ad hoc necessity, and Brahman, the *prakṛti* of *prakṛti*, can conceal mind in matter and matter in mind: against Russell's neutral monism, mind is material and matter is mental (*sarvaṁ sarvamayam*). After the commentary on *prāṇa* and the *rājasa* intellect and a note on realism, conventionalism and surrealism, he begins the *tanmātras* and *bhūtas* (3.26.32): *bhūta* is being confined to a given place and time, space is itself matter and is present in all matter, and sound is of two kinds, *dhvani* and *varṇa*, the articulate syllables being imposed on sound by the hearer.
