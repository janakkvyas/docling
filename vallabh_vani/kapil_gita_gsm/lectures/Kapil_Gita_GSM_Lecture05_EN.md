And what he asked — what she asked:

# 2. Mahāprabhuji's Kārikās: Yoga as the Support of the Citta

*Antarātmā svayaṁ cittam indriyāṇi tathā tanuḥ; vede sāṅkhye ca yoge ca śaive vaiṣṇava eva ca; mūla-rūpāṇi śāstrāṇāṁ niyamārthaṁ nirūpaṇāt* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. *Ātma-śeṣo hy ahaṅkāraḥ sa sāṅkhye vinirūpyate* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: and self-awareness, subordinate to the *ātmā*, soul, which is described in the Sāṅkhya. *Ekīkṛtya manaś cittaṁ yogo yatra pravartate*: and all this, including all this, *manas* etcetera — *cittaṁ yogo hy atra pravartate*. ‹…› The *śāstrārtha*, the subject matter of *śāstra*, is divided into two form, knowledge and action. *Cittasya rūpa-bhedāś ca yad yac ca rūpam ihocitam, tādṛśasya phalaṁ cāpi sādhanāni bahūni ca* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. And different forms of the *citta*; *yac ca rūpam ihocitam*: and those forms which are ‹…› and which are good to be described here; *tādṛśasya phalaṁ cāpi*: and the reward of those, that type of *citta*; and *sādhanāni bahūni ca*: and various means to achieve that *citta*. *Tatrāpi ekasya nirdhāras tasya sādhanam eva ca; tasyāpi viṣayaḥ proktaś caturbhiḥ ṣoḍaśātmakaḥ* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. And there, there too, the object of the *citta* described by four, four *kārikās*. *Tasya sādhanatā-rūpaṁ tathā tv ayaṁ mokṣa-sādhane* ‹?›: and that is this instrumental; that is the instrument for acquiring liberation. *Baliṣṭhaṁ tu phalaṁ siddhet, tasmād iti vinirṇayaḥ* ‹?›: and if it is powerful enough, then it may provide fruits, reward, result.

# 5. All the Senses in a Race for God's Work; Bhāva, Anya-bhāva and Ananya-bhāva

*[00:22:09]*

**Speaker:** *Evaṁ sarva-doṣa-parihāram uktvā teṣāṁ sarva-puruṣārtha-sādhakatva-siddhy-arthaṁ svasminn ādhidaivika-guṇān āha* ‹?›. Like this, by removing all *doṣas* — *sarva-sādhaka*, *sarva-puruṣārtha-sādhakatva-siddhy-artham* — because this yoga becomes the provider of all the *puruṣārtha*, pursuits, reward of pursuits, Kapila Ṛṣi, Kapila Bhagavān, describing his own divine qualities. *Bhaktir agre vaktavyā*: *bhakti* he will describe not here, but in the coming stanzas.

*Sarvendriyāṇāṁ manasā saha ahamahamikayā bhagavat-kārya-paratvam iti.* Now this is a key concept: *sarvendriyāṇāṁ manasā saha ahamahamikayā bhagavat-kārya-paratvam*. All our sense organs and motor organs should be in race. *Aham-ahamikā* means: I am first, I am first, I am first; let me go first. And when they become *bhagavat-kārya-para*, when they employ themselves for the cause of God — *indriyāṇi ca nirduṣṭāni saguṇāni ca iti*. Because these *indriya*, sense organs and motor organs, they become *nirduṣṭāni*. They are of both nature, *nirduṣṭāni* and *saguṇāni ca*. Because what is the — by themselves they are not *nirdoṣa*, *sadoṣa*; but what they look for make them either *sadoṣa* or *nirdoṣa*, with fault or without fault.

*Na vidyate 'nyasmin yo bhāvaḥ*: so when the inclination for others is reduced. *Bhāvo nāma antaḥkaraṇa-dharmo 'bhiprāya-paryāyaḥ* ‹?›, *sarvatra hetuḥ; sa bhakti-janako 'pi bhavati*. # Brief Summary

**Content.** The session opens the twenty-fifth chapter. Devahūti, weary of sense-gratification and recalling Brahmā's promise, asks her son Kapila to cut the tree of "me and mine" and to teach her about *prakṛti* and *puruṣa* (3.25.1–12). Through Mahāprabhuji's *kārikās* the speaker shows that yoga is taught here as the support of the *citta*, and Kapila's yoga (3.25.13–27) is read through: the mind binds when attached to the *guṇas* and frees when turned to the self; purified of *kāma* and *lobha* it sees the self beyond *prakṛti*; no path is more auspicious than *bhakti* to the Lord; attachment turned to the *sādhus* opens the door of liberation, and their company brings *śraddhā*, *rati* and *bhakti* in order. The Subodhinī draws out the race of all the senses for God's work, *bhāva* as intention, and *ananya-bhāva* as the exclusion of other gods, things, paths and rewards; renunciation is for *sevā* and not from lethargy, and relatives who are devotees need not be left. Devahūti then asks what *bhakti* suits her (3.25.28–31), and Kapila defines it (3.25.32–33): every sense has a divine and a demonic urge, the demonic stronger; control comes through *prāṇa* and *prāṇāyāma*; the divine senses are known by their objects, are free of *laya* and *vikṣepa*, and are engaged in the *ānuśravika*, Vedic, *karma*; the natural, unmotivated *vṛtti*, or commitment, of a one-pointed mind toward the *guṇātīta* Lord is *bhakti*, *bhāgavatī* because it takes on his six attributes, greater than *siddhi* and than *sāyujya*, and it digests the subtle body as fire digests food. The fruits of *sevā* (3.25.34–36) follow: devotees who want no merging, whose *bhakti* has become *rasa*, who see his forms and speak with him, and who are given *sāyujya* unasked; the four *puruṣārthas* are fulfilled in God's limbs, play, smiling glance and loving speech, as in the Catuḥślokī. In answer to questions, the speaker explains that Kapila mixes yoga and *bhakti* for his mother's sake, that Mahāprabhuji took only the devotion from this passage, that *sāyujya* in Puṣṭimārga keeps the devotee's *rasa*, as in Yamunā, and that Brahmavāda is for Mahāprabhuji a teleological, not an ontological, doctrine.

**Examples and Stories.** "Aham-ahamikā": all the senses racing, each saying "let me go first"; the eye removed at the sight of something foul; prāṇāyāma tried and untried in meditation, and the half-closed eye of Gosāīṁjī's *ardhonmīlitākṣa* and the Gītā's gaze between the brows; both urges present in one eyeball, even in those of *daivī sṛṣṭi*; the senses lost in a very attractive stimulus; the Veda called *anuśrava* because it was heard from the guru before there were books; Pañcāgni Vidyā likened to the nitrogen cycle; food digested without any further effort; Prahlāda's natural commitment; Mīrā's "*līno mol*"; donors in the *havelīs* called *bhāgavata* while the true *bhāgavata* is one who belongs to God; *vāma* and the left side of the beloved; the speaker's artist uncle in Mathurā who walked out of the Taj Mahal when a visitor called it mere marble and carving, and then showed the cousins its angles as an embodied beloved; fountains changing colour and pattern as *vilāsa*; Māgha's "*kṣaṇe kṣaṇe yan navatām upaiti*"; the mother wearied by a crying child and excited by a playful one; Immanuel Kant's sense of ought; contagious laughter, mimicry and yawning; *unmāda* and *pramāda*; the Lord as the butter-thief and the foremost of thieves who steals the senses; the Braj *pada* "*kāhe ko deh damat sādhan kar*"; Yamunā as Kṛṣṇa's feminine form who still calls herself his devotee; ornament and gold; Mahāprabhuji in the Vedastuti Subodhinī preferring Buddhist *śūnyatā* to a Brahmavāda that would discourage devotion.
