# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session completes the Subodhinī's treatment of *citta* and moves through the four verses that define *ahaṅkāra* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.22–26). After a grammatical opening — *cit* with the *ta* suffix gives *citta*, like *gata* and *huta* — the speaker reads the ādhibhautika definition of *citta* by its *vṛttis*, *svacchatva*, *avikāritva* and *śāntatva*, and Mahāprabhuji's reason for three qualifiers: each excludes one of the other inner faculties, *buddhi* being *viṣayākāra*, *manas* always *vikārātmaka*, and *ahaṅkāra* *śānta* only in its *sāttvika* phase while *citta* is always *śānta*. The objection — if *citta* is pure by nature, why *yoga* as *citta-vṛtti-nirodha*, and why *sevā* defined as *cetas tat-pravaṇam* — is met by the example of water: waves, bubbles and mud come from wind and earth and are *aupādhika*; a *lakṣaṇa* must be *akṛtrima*, stated from *sahaja svabhāva*, which is also why *bhakti* must be defined without *upādhi* and why *upādhi-bhakti* diminishes when wishes fail or multiply.
