So this is the *puruṣa*: the *ātmā* is *pratyag-dhāmā*. It's confined to the body where it resides.

# 4. Verse 4 — sa eṣa vibhuḥ: Prakṛti Sūkṣmā, Daivī, Guṇamayī; the Director behind the Screen

*[00:17:07]*

**Speaker:** So *anādi*, *nirguṇa*, *prakṛteḥ para*: is neither *saguṇa*, nor it's — nor *sāttvika*, *rājasa* or *tāmasa*; it's *nirguṇa*. *Prakṛteḥ para*: it's above *prakṛti*, because the bodily aspects, which are formed by *prakṛti*, will take birth, will remain and will die; but the *cetanā* is not bound by that time frame. *Pratyag-dhāmā*: but it shines in body only; our *cetanā* resides in the body and is confined to the body only. *Svayaṁ-jyoti*: it's self-illuminating; it does not require any other illumination. *Viśvaṁ yena samanvitam.*

*Sa eṣa puruṣaḥ vibhuḥ* — is all-capable. Due to its quality it is all-capable; *vibhu* means all-capable. *Sa eṣa vibhuḥ sūkṣmāṁ daivīṁ guṇamayīṁ prakṛtim* —

> स एष प्रकृतिं सूक्ष्मां दैवीं गुणमयीं विभुः ।  
> यदृच्छयैवोपगतामभ्यपद्यत लीलया ॥  
>
> *sa eṣa prakṛtiṁ sūkṣmāṁ daivīṁ guṇa-mayīṁ vibhuḥ |*  
> *yadṛcchayaivopagatām abhyapadyata līlayā ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.4)
>
> [This puruṣa, vibhu, all-capable, combines — in a manner of sports, līlayā — with prakṛti, which is sūkṣmā, subtle, daivī, having that type of quality which are superhuman, and guṇamayī, nothing but combination of sattva, raja and tama, and which accidentally comes near it.]

How is *prakṛti* *sūkṣmā*? Because the nature — we do come across all the natural products, but nature itself is, just like a director, acts behind the screen. All his direction you can visualize — everything, actors acting — but director directing you will not be able to visualize; you will not be able to see directly. So — but everything is directed by director, but he remains behind the screen. Like that, everything is produced by *prakṛti*, and that *prakṛti* product, which are called *vikṛti* or *vikāra*, they are visible; but *prakṛti* itself is *sūkṣma*. So *sūkṣma*, so subtle: just like — everything is made up of electron, proton and neutron, but they are not visible; their combination is visible in form of different elements — understood? — and many a times the element form the molecules of different compound, they are visible; not even — some, many a times, elements are not visible that way. So same way *prakṛti* is *sūkṣma*. It is *daivī*: it has all that type of quality which are superhuman. *Guṇamayī* — third aspect is *prakṛti* is *guṇamayī*: *prakṛti* is nothing but combination of *sattva*, *raja* and *tama*; so all the three *guṇas* — these three *guṇas*, *sattva*, *raja* and *tama*, are there which constitutes the *prakṛti* — so *guṇamayī*. And *yadṛcchayaiva upagatām*: by accidentally it comes near *puruṣa*; and *puruṣa* — what, if *puruṣa* does? *Abhyapadyata līlayā*, *abhyapadyata līlayā*: with, in a manner of ‹sports›, it combines with *prakṛti*. You can call *abhyapadyata līlayā*: it acquires, it combines — it combines with the *prakṛti*. *Abhyapadyata līlayā.* And what, when the *puruṣa* comes in contact and combines with *prakṛti*, what happens?

# 5. Verse 5 — guṇair vicitrāḥ: Three Threads, the Shades of Green, vicitra as vividha citra

> गुणैर्विचित्राः सृजतीं सरूपाः प्रकृतिं प्रजाः ।

>
> *guṇair vicitrāḥ sṛjatīṁ sa-rūpāḥ prakṛtiṁ prajāḥ |*

>

Then actually you can visualize: if there are three threads — just in Gujarati it is: red, yellow and blue, main colour is said; the rest are all obtained from that — you can visualize there are three types, three threads of these three colours; and if they are interwoven in such a manner that it can produce many design — peacocks and parrots and this and that, what not; whole world you can create by combination of these three threads. But when you keep these three thread, you don't see any design, anything, no product. But when they are activated, then it starts its production. And how it starts its production? *Guṇair vicitrāḥ*: how strange it is, *sṛṣṭi* is, that everything is unique! In *prakṛti*, how it is *līlā* that everything is unique. Everything is unique. Many a times, due to commonality, we consider as a one: say, leaves are green. When we say in a common manner, it's also true — leaves are green. But in Sanskrit *vicitra* means *vividha citra*: the same thing, but has got so much — so much distinctively different from one another — that is *vicitra*: so much multicoloured, multi-figured. Yes. # 7. Verses 6–7 — parābhidhyāna and saṁsṛti-bandha: Milk and Water, the Servant Who Becomes Master, Anaesthesia

> कर्मसु क्रियमाणेषु गुणैरात्मनि मन्यते ॥  
>
> *evaṁ parābhidhyānena kartṛtvaṁ prakṛteḥ pumān |*  
> *karmasu kriyamāṇeṣu guṇair ātmani manyate ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.6)
>

# Brief Summary

**Content.** The tenth session resumes at Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.3 and carries the exposition through 3.26.10. After restating the two fundamentals of Sāṅkhya — puruṣa as pure, inactive sensitivity and prakṛti as the active, material aspect — the speaker unfolds the four epithets of verse 3: the cetanā can hold the whole universe conceptually (viśvaṁ yena samanvitam), it is confined to the body it occupies (pratyag-dhāmā), it is self-illuminating and therefore the one thing even radical doubt cannot doubt (svayaṁ-jyoti), and it is above prakṛti and its time frame.
