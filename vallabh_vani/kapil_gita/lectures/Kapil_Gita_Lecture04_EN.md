# 6. Sāṅkhya Is Tyāga, Yoga Is the Reverse Journey; the Three Yogas and Their Adhikārīs

*[00:20:27]*

**Speaker:** So first, *yogānusāra-śāstrārtha-nirūpaṇam*. Here, *sāṅkhya-parityāga*. What happens in Sāṅkhya, that we can understand from Bālabodha also. In Bālabodha it is said: *tyāgātyāga-vibhāgena sāṅkhye tyāgaḥ parikīrtitaḥ* — *tyāgātyāga-vibhāgena sāṅkhya*: in that *svāśraya* — in case of *svāśraya* there is *sāṅkhya* and *yoga*; in case of *sāṅkhya* there is *tyāga* — is there: you have to leave everything. In *jñāna mārga* you have to leave everything, and then only you will have true knowledge. So *sa tyāgātyāga-vibhāgena sāṅkhye tyāgaḥ parikīrtitaḥ, atyāge yoga-mārgo hi, tyāgo 'pi mānasaiva hi*‹?› ‹श्रवणाधारित›. In *yoga mārga* there is not *tyāga*: you have not to leave everything, you have not to take *sannyāsa*, but you have to retreat yourself; you have to travel a reverse journey, inside from outside. That is the importance of *yoga*. So Mahāprabhuji says *sāṅkhya-parityāgo nitya iti tad anuktvā* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. That's why — Devahūti is *mamatā* — one who is having more inclination of *ahantā*, how Bhagavān says in Bhāgavata:

> योगास्त्रयो मया प्रोक्ता नृणां श्रेयोविधित्सया ।  
> ज्ञानं कर्म च भक्तिश्च नोपायोऽन्योऽस्ति कुत्रचित् ॥  
>
> *yogās trayo mayā proktā nṝṇāṁ śreyo-vidhitsayā |*  
> *jñānaṁ karma ca bhaktiś ca nopāyo 'nyo 'sti kutracit ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 11.20.6)

> *nirviṇṇānāṁ jñāna-yogo nyāsinām iha karmasu |*

>
> [*Jñāna-yoga* is for the *nirviṇṇa*: the *adhikārī* of *jñāna* is one who is having very less *mamatā*; and *karma-yoga* is for the *kāmī*, one who is having *kāmanā* — this I want, that I want.]

So *karma-yogas tu kāminām*. Means — basically your inclination is there: you are more inclined on *ahantā*, you are having very less *mamatā*; so it become easy to remove that *mamatā*. Be concentrated on your *ahantā*, and after that — that *ahantā*, you do *upāsanā* and everything, and then you do *ahaṁ brahmāsmi*, and you can go, move towards *jñāna mārga*. So *adhikārī* of *jñāna* is one who is having very less *mamatā*, who is — nowadays what — what happens? Everybody is self-centred. But in spite of being self-centred, they are more *mamatāspada*. But which *mamatā*? *Mamatā* towards *bhoga*. They are not self-centred person, because their self — self means body; here self means body. Everybody is — whatever suits our body, we want that. Individual freedom, individual this, individual that, individual that — that is self-centredness. But self — that self-centredness is — we can call it *ahantā*; that is *ahantā* only related to the body. You forget about your *ātmā*, or any soul, or any other thing — *ātmā* or any other thing. But in case of our tradition, you see, those who are having *ahantā*, more inclined towards *ahantā*, they are concentrated on themselves — themselves means the *ātmā* — and they can easily leave the *mamatā*. So they are prone, they are possible, that they can easily have a *vairāgya* and go, do *tyāga*: have *vairāgya* first, after *vairāgya* do *tyāga*, and go for *jñāna mārga*. So *nirviṇṇānāṁ jñāna-yogaḥ*.

And one who is having *kāmanā* — say, this I want, that I want, this I want, the everything. What — what we don't want? If we don't like, then we don't want; that is a different thing. That is our nurture, how we are nurtured: we like something, we don't like that thing. But whatever we like, we want it. That is our desire, that is our *kāmanā*. And nobody — they can — we can hide our *kāmanā*, just like *nā milī nārī to bābā brahmacārī* [when no woman was to be had, Bābā turned *brahmacārī*] — like that we can hide our *kāmanā* inside. But we don't know; just we have suppressed, like spring; when it will come back with a full speed, we don't know. So *karma-yogas tu kāminām*. That's why our Bhagavān says you employ *karma yoga*, means *yajña*, and that you share the thing with — not only with the human being, but whole universe: *devatā*, *prāṇi-mātra* and everything — *pañca-mahāyajña*, as we discussed in *bhakti yoga*. You have to do *karma yoga*. So in — when you do — and:

> स्वे स्वे कर्मण्यभिरतः संसिद्धिं लभते नरः ।  
>
> *sve sve karmaṇy abhirataḥ saṁsiddhiṁ labhate naraḥ |* (Bhagavadgītā 18.45)

You are keep on performing your duty, self-duty, and by performing your self-duty — *sve sve karmaṇy abhirataḥ saṁsiddhiṁ labhate naraḥ*.
