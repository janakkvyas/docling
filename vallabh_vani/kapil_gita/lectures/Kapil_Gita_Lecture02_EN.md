# 1. The Problem of Sādhana and Phala: When the Reward Is Already Promised

*[00:00:01]*

**Speaker:** Jai Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Hello, any question?

**A student:** Ah, no, no questions.

**Speaker:** Okay. Wait for two minutes and then we will start. Okay.

**A student:** Okay.

**Speaker:** Then we'll start. Last time we have seen the text of third and fourth śloka; Subodhinījī we have to see. Here the problem is raised that usually — what is the relation between *sādhana* and *phala*? Usually, whenever you accomplish your means — means are accomplished perfectly — then you get the reward. In our case the *phala* is also of two types. One is reward also: whatever you like to get. And *phala* is also your — the destiny, result, say *karma-phala*. You may like it or you may not like it, but at the end it is: whatever you have done, you will get the *karma-phala*. So that is one type of describing nature. Here, in our Bhakti-mārga, it is — because we relish it, it is our reward. So Bhagavān himself is our reward. When he himself has — he become pleased on Kardama ṛṣi and he blessed them that "I'll be your son, I'll come to your home as a son" — then what is the now use of doing all *sādhanas*? Say — usually, just now also, whenever exams are over we do not refer to the books again. Why to refer the books? To appear the exam we do read the books, and then after exams, "Oh, oh, what's the need to refer the books, to read the books?" Usually that type of relation is there: whenever the reward, or whatever we want, is achieved, then there is no use of means. Isn't it? That is usual practice. So what is the role of *sādhana*, or the means, in Puṣṭi Bhakti-mārga? That is beautifully discussed here by Śrī Mahāprabhuji: ‹…› *sādhanaṁ na kartavyam iti* ‹āha› ‹श्रवणाधारित› —

> धृतव्रतासि भद्रं ते दमेन नियमेन च ।  
> तपोद्रविणदानैश्च श्रद्धया चेश्वरं भज ॥  
>
> *dhṛta-vratāsi bhadraṁ te damena niyamena ca |*  
> *tapo-draviṇa-dānaiś ca śraddhayā ceśvaraṁ bhaja ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.24.3)
>
> [*Dhṛtavratāsi* — you have taken the *vrata*; *bhadraṁ te* — let be your *kalyāṇa*; by these means, *damena niyamena tapo-draviṇa-dānaiś ca śraddhayā ca*, you worship, you do *bhakti* of Īśvara.]

After all — by all this, when we use the *tṛtīyā*, *kāraṇārthaka tṛtīyā* [the instrumental case, in the sense of the means] — means *damena niyamena tapo-draviṇa-dānaiḥ ca īśvaraṁ bhaja*. That is the principal sentence. Principal sentence — the *ākhyāta* is *bhaja*. ‹*Kam*› *bhaja*? *Īśvaraṁ bhaja*. *Kena bhaja*? *Damena niyamena tapo-draviṇa-dānena śraddhayā ca*. By these means you worship, you do *bhakti* of Īśvara. So, after all, whatever *sādhanas* are there — first indication is that whatever *sādhana* in Bhakti-mārga we incur, we do, whatever means we employ, all become part of *bhakti*. That we have discussed in — further; Jeje has also given introduction of Kapila Gītā; there — there also he has discussed this. So that all becomes part of *bhakti-sādhana*; all *sādhanas* become *aṅga*.

# 2. *Sādhanāni siddhe bhagavati pravartante*: Sādhana Makes Bhagavān Known and Available

*[00:05:04]*

**Speaker:** But here, when the reward is going to come himself, by — without, without any requirement of means, what is the use of means? — is the problem. When the one above gives, gives by tearing the roof, like that he himself is coming. Then what is the use of this means? That Mahāprabhuji explained: *sādhanāni siddhe bhagavati pravartante* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Now you are waiting for Bhagavān, and he has admitted, he has given, he has blessed you that he is going to come. That was what — reminded; Kardama ṛṣi reminded Devahūti, which was — who was cursing herself; say, "Don't curse you; we are pious, and Bhagavān himself is going to come as your son, so why to curse? But do the *sādhana*." So what is the *upayoga*, what is the role of *sādhana*? Let's say when Bhagavān is going to come, then also we have to do: *sādhanāni siddhe bhagavati pravartante*. *Yadā bhagavān svayam eva āgamiṣyati* — he is not coming due to any of this; otherwise it would have been *sādhana-mārga*‹?›. If he is coming due to the *tapa*, *dāna*, *śraddhā*, then it is a different case. He has already admitted, he has given blessing that he is going to come; so he is not coming due to any of the means. But *yadā bhagavān svayam eva āgamiṣyāmi iti manyate* ‹श्रवणाधारित› — Bhagavān is going to come himself, because he, by his —

> नायमात्मा प्रवचनेन लभ्यो न मेधया न बहुना श्रुतेन ।  
> यमेवैष वृणुते तेन लभ्यः …  
>
> *nāyam ātmā pravacanena labhyo na medhayā na bahunā śrutena |*  
> *yam evaiṣa vṛṇute tena labhyaḥ …* (Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.2.23, as far as recited)
>
> [This *ātmā* is not to be gained by discourse, nor by intellect, nor by much hearing; whom he chooses, by him he is gained.]

So that way he is going to come himself, without waiting, or without seeing the — whatever means are there. But what is the role of *sādhana*? That is explained here, that *sādhanāni kṛtāni bhagavantaṁ bodhayanti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. First of all you need to understand, to have knowledge of Bhagavān; otherwise Bhagavān, even if he is available, becomes unavailable. So both is required: proper knowledge is required, proper understanding is required. So when you do *sādhana* — many a times what happens, just as we are learning, say, *kṛṣṇa-sevā sadā kāryā mānasī sā parā matā* (Siddhāntamuktāvalī 1) — everything we are learning. Now whatever we are learning, say — but if we do it, one type of understanding is generated, that "I have to do it". One is theoretical understanding and one is practical understanding. Practical understanding — that theoretical understanding, "I should do it"; but practical understanding comes only if you practice it. So if you do *sādhana* for Bhagavān, that means you — *bodha* of Bhagavān is generated; and not only that, Bhagavān becomes available by that only: *sādhanāni bhagavantaṁ prāpnuvanti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Otherwise he is with you, but you forget him; is with you, but is not available. Mahāprabhuji beautifully says that many a times, because of our nature of *jaḍa prakṛti*, we keep on repeating so many things, and we keep on repeating so many things, it becomes mechanical, and the heart — is the main gist; or heart is lost in our mechanical repetition. Many a times such thing happens, do happens, in many — the core of religion, core of any doing — what is the main aspect or main purpose gets lost, many a times. So it is said that *bhagavantaṁ prāpnuvanti*: though Bhagavān is — he is not *prāpta* by any *sādhana*, but in response to coming of Bhagavān, if you are doing any *sādhana*, then that *sādhana* shows that you are waiting for Bhagavān, you are longing for Bhagavān; and that makes Bhagavān available to you. Otherwise many times we have seen in our Puṣṭi Bhakti Sampradāya that though they are doing Bhagavat-sevā, Kṛṣṇa-sevā, but still they do not think that "I am doing Kṛṣṇa-sevā" — the importance of Kṛṣṇa-sevā, or importance of the ‹*sevya*›, they do not feel. So if you do *sādhana*, then you will feel; that it makes Bhagavān available to you — *prāpnuvanti*. Not only that: whatever Bhagavān has — Bhagavān is with you, is going to be with you; in our case it is with us, but in case of Devahūti is going to come, surely is going to come. But what *sādhana* will do: whatever these *sādhanas* are done, they will — they will make him under your control.

# 3. *Vaśīkurvanti, utpādayanti*: The Sweet Control of Love and the Bhāvātmaka Svarūpa

*[00:11:05]*

**Speaker:** *Vaśīkurvanti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: not only you will be able to worship him, but Bhagavān has got that type of inclination, that those —

> समोऽहं सर्वभूतेषु न मे द्वेष्योऽस्ति न प्रियः ।  
> ये भजन्ति तु मां भक्त्या मयि ते तेषु चाप्यहम् ॥  
>
> *samo 'haṁ sarva-bhūteṣu na me dveṣyo 'sti na priyaḥ |*  
> *ye bhajanti tu māṁ bhaktyā mayi te teṣu cāpy aham ||* (Bhagavad Gītā 9.29)
>
> [I am the same to all beings; none is hateful to me, none dear; but those who worship me with *bhakti* — they are in me, and I too am in them.]

So Bhagavān is *bhakta-bhaktimān*. So as — *bhakta* is *bhaktimān*; so in *bhakti*, *bhaktimān* — what, what happens? The nature of love is that it — you are controlled by Bhagavān; then only you are loving. But you are controlled; you are — when, whenever the independence is there, there is no bad thing about it; but whenever you are in love of anything, it controls you; and if it does not control you, then you are not loving it. If it controls you, then — but that is sweet control. That — that way, same, Bhagavān is also *bhakta-bhaktimān*. So you can then, doing this *sādhana*, you can control him also. Not only; then you can produce Bhagavān. How you can produce Bhagavān? Say, previously, when Bhagavān Viṣṇu, Śukla Nārāyaṇa, gave *darśana* to Kardamajī, he was not Devahūti-putra, Devahūti-nandana *yā* [or] Kardama-nandana. But now, after taking — in our, our case the glory of *avatāra* is that now onwards he — you will be the producer; you will give birth to Kapiladeva. So *vaśīkurvanti*; not only that, but *utpādayanti* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Same way Bhagavat-rūpa is *bhāvātmaka*, and that *bhāvātmaka rūpa* is produced by — so if Roman‹?› has thought negatively of us, that we are stone-worshippers, then idol-worshippers — so they thought, and "that's why we are creating beautiful temples and worshipping the idols"; but that's why they thought, and they destroyed the idols. So they thought that "we will have no *śraddhā* in either", because, just like them, we thought — just like that they did in Kaba: they destroyed the whatever idols they were worshipping, and the whole — the clan was converted; because now, whatever you are dependent on — some idol, some *mūrti* — and that is destroyed, so it is destructible; how to depend on it? So that way they succeeded — not Mughals, but later on British succeeded little bit of it, that way, by generating this type of nuance: that if you are dependent on whom is just lowly, that it can be destroyed, how he will save you? That is — that type of nuance they have created ‹the speaker's characterization›. But when you say *bhāvātmaka* — so, but in previous case Mughals killed, and all the barbarians, all the Mongolian barbarians, destroyed nearly thirty-forty thousand temples and idols which we were worshipping, but could not hamper any of our *śraddhā* ‹the speaker's characterization›. Because *śraddhā* — it is *viṣaya* of *śraddhā*. For those who do not have *śraddhā*, it is just mere stone or wood or anything; but for those who are having *śraddhā*, he is not just stone; he is the one residing in the heart; and the one who is — reside, *bhāvātmaka svarūpa*, is there in our heart; and who is the creator of that *bhāvātmaka svarūpa* is the one who is having that *bhāva*.

# 4. Not a Cab to Be Left: *Svayam eva*, the Sword-Marriage and Pratīka Upāsanā

*[00:15:30]*

**Speaker:** So not only — beautifully, as explained Mahāprabhuji, that *tadā yadā bhagavān svayam eva āgamiṣyāmi* ‹श्रवणाधारित›: this is not that nature of *sādhana* and *phala*, that you do *sādhana* and as a reward you get the *phala*, and when you get the *phala* you say, "Whatever the *sādhanas* are required — no, no usage." Explains like this: you take a cab, you reach the place where you want to go, and then leave the cab, just like that. But if you do your own car, then you won't be able to leave it; you will keep it nicely. So that way, whatever *sādhanas* are there, here the relation is another thing. Whatever fruit we are getting — there are some — here, *bhagavān svayam eva āgamiṣyāmi*. Why it is said *svayam eva āgamiṣyāmi*? So whatever we worship is the token, *pratīka*; it is shown as *pratīka-upāsanā*; we just — token of it, we worship the token of it. So that sword was just token. So he can't say that "I have — I have married sword; I have not married you." So it is just token. So that is *pratīka-upāsanā*. That *pratīka-upāsanā* is there, just like — no, Jeje said, "Kill Buddha, if Buddha comes, distracts your meditation" — that last time he explained, no? Last time only he explained, no?

**Speaker:** So that only said: that is a *pratīka-upāsanā*, means you want to go to higher place; just treat it as a *pratīka*, with reverence; you call it *pratīka*, but in a one way it is just stepping stone. That's why a tendency comes that if *pratīka* disturbs you to reach a higher level, and if you get stuck to the *pratīka* — then the *pratīka* means symbol; if you get stuck to it, then you are nowhere; so you will not reach where you want to go. In our case also it is Kṛṣṇa-sevā: what *sevā* you are doing is of Kṛṣṇa only, not of anyone else; he is *svayam*. So *bhagavān svayam eva āgamiṣyati*.

# 5. Not for Ātmoddhāra: Bhagavān Has Jumped into the Well, and Sādhana Is the Wedding Gift

*[00:19:26]*

**Speaker:** But still, what is the use of — so many a times whatever means you employ, you employ because the — this world is bondage; everything is — we are engulfed in *māyā*; we can't come out of ourselves. So for *ātmoddhāra*, for uplifting ourselves, we have to do Bhagavān — Bhagavat-bhakti, all the *sādhanas* we do. But here we do not do it for *ātmoddhāra*, because the notion of *ātmoddhāra* is to uplift the *ātmā*, uplifted *ātmā*. But Mahāprabhuji says beautifully that whenever you see some — someone who you — whom you know has fallen somewhere, say in well or river, something — what you will do? You will — you will get some rope and throw it there and drive them out of river or well. And — but if you are more near and dear, and if you are well capable of swimming and taking the load, what you will do? You will not wait even for bringing rope or anything; you will jump there and you will bring them back on the place. So same way, we may be anywhere, but what is the Siddhāntarahasya? Bhagavān himself has jumped in our *saṁsāra*. So we are less worried about *ātmoddhāra*. Because what happens if Bhagavān comes to our place, and say we — we say, "No, no, no, we don't — you don't — you have come here? We were coming to your place. So you go back to your place." So like this tragedy would happen: that "Why you came here? We only — we want to come to your place, no, not here." So if somebody, guest, comes to our place and we treat like this — "No, no, no, we were go— we have decided to come to your place, so go back to your place, and we are going to come over there" — that type of fiasco will be there. But Bhagavān *svayam* — he has come to us. So the nature of *sādhana* has totally change, and *ekātmatā* of *sādhana* and *phala* is also created. So now whatever we want to do, we want to do to please Bhagavān. To please Bhagavān — to please, not to — for coming.

That is explained here: *yathā loke svabhāvato bhoktāram atithi-nimantraṇādinā vaśīkṛtya bhojayanti, na tu abhoktāram upāya-śatair api* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. If somebody likes to have some nice food in dinner or lunch, then you can — then only you can invite and give party of lunch or dinner or any party. Otherwise, if he is not enjoying any food — say, by chance, if he is doing strict diet and he is enjoying only restricted food, and whatever food item he likes he prepares by himself at his place — then how you will invite him? And how you will be able to serve him nicely? Won't be able to serve him. But one who likes to — who has inclination, who likes being served — then only you can serve him. Isn't it? So *loke svabhāvataḥ* — in normal case also, whatever *bhoktā*, *bhoktā* are there — one wants to have some — have some festivity, wants to have some good party — then only you can invite them and enjoy, serve them and enjoy in that serving. So *svabhāvato bhoktāram atithi-nimantraṇādinā vaśīkṛtya bhojayanti, na tu abhoktāram upāya-śataiḥ* — by thousands of — if one is not enjoying, by thousands of things you will not be able to please him; *upāya-śatair api*. *Yato bhagavān svayam eva āgantā* — Bhagavān is coming himself — *ataḥ sādhanāni kartavyāni* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. Now so, in response to that — so whatever nature of *sādhana* is there, that if somebody wants to come to our place, it's not like that he is coming for that, or he will be pleased by that only. But what is the response of our heart if somebody comes to our place? Say, just like that, in marriage or any other things, why the system of gift came? Gift maybe small rose or small thing or anything — why it came? As if we are funding the whole marriage or whole thing? No, we are not funding it; that is a different nature of funding. But we are just expressing the pleasure; our pleasure gets expressed in some — in form of some gift, or rose, or some — any other thing. So that is nothing but our love, and our pleasure for that festivity, is expressed in form of that gift. Same way, in case of Bhagavān, whatever *sādhana* we are doing, we are always appreciating coming of Bhagavān, and in response to that we are compelled to do something. If somebody comes to our place, he may be very of high position; and with that, narration of *bhaktas* are there — of Śabarī, of Vidurajī, and all other things; they — of Śrutadeva and all other things you can read, we can read; in — of Prahlāda also we can read in Bhāgavata-śāstra, that what he can do. He said — what he say? Say, he said, "I am son of *asura* and I am five years old. What praise I can do it, when this Brahmā and all are singing your glory — are not attending to it, not heeding to it — what can I do it? What I — what I can offer you? What — how I can praise you? But still, as you have come for me, I am singing your glory; I am doing your *stotra*; I am doing your *prārthanā*, not — not for any other things." And he said, "You are my unconditional *svāmī* and I am your unconditional ‹*sevaka*›." That unconditional — it is there, always remains there in the nature of *sādhana*. *Sādhana* do not come as a condition. That nature of *sādhana* and *phala* of condition relation is a different case, other than the Bhakti-mārga, this *śuddha* Bhakti-mārga. So *bhagavān yathā* — *bhavān bhagavān svayam samāgantā, ataḥ sādhanāni kartavyāni* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. So in response to Bhagavān we have to give some response, and our heart is compelled to give some response; and that response is expressed in form of *sādhana*.

# 6. *Dhṛtavratāsi*: Pativrata, *Mātṛdevo bhava* and Kardama's Blessing

*[00:27:29]*

**Speaker:** *Tatra kānicit deha-śuddhyartham ādau kartavyāni; iti tava na kartavyāni iti āha — dhṛtavratāsi* ‹श्रवणाधारित›. In our culture, first we have to purify ourselves by *snānādi*; that is the first *sādhana*. But you have not to do that, because what you have taken — *pativrata*. Means *pativrata* means worshipping *pati* as the Bhagavān; and in nowadays — which is of very old custom. But it's not like that: *pati* only *deva*. It is our — in our culture, whenever student — studies are finished, and goes from *brahmacarya-āśrama* to *gṛhastha-āśrama*, the guru, *ācārya*, always says — says to him: *mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava* (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2). And means — it's not like that *mātā* is *deva*, *pitā* is *deva*; they are human being, just of our nature only, and many times of some lower or some greater nature — *unnīs-bīs* [nineteen-twenty: much the same] only, not much difference. But you be like that. You be like that, that for you your *mātā* is *deva*; then the nature of relation will be very pious. You be like that: your *pitā* is *deva*. *Pitṛ* — *tvam* — *pitṛdevo bhava*: it is *bahuvrīhi samāsa*, *pitṛdevo bhava*. Just like, for example, we can understand: *daśānana* means ten heads. Ten heads means what? Ten people come together — we can say, can we say *daśānana*, in Sanskrit? You count the head: one *ānana*, two *ānana*, three *ānana*, and ten — ten heads. So, ten heads. But here *daśānana* is *bahuvrīhi samāsa*. *Bahuvrīhi samāsa* means what? *Daśa ānanāni — daśa ānanāni yasya saḥ*: one who is having ten heads, that is Rāvaṇa. Pītāmbara is also of two nature. Pītāmbara is — the *ambara* means the cloth; yellow cloth, bright yellow cloth, is also called *pītāmbara*; and Bhagavān — name of Bhagavān is also Pītāmbara. How Bhagavān name is Pītāmbara? He is not cloth. But *pītam ambaraṁ yasya saḥ*: one who is wearing *pītāmbara*, one who is wearing bright yellow cloth, shining yellow cloth, he is also called as Pītāmbara. Like that: *mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava*. So that was a custom to worship the — this is now old-fashioned thing, so we do not find it suitable. But it is just — even *atithi*, whom you don't know — one may be of anyone, anyone, any good or bad or anyone — but just as your household person, anybody has come to your place, treat him as *deva*. Don't — don't provide the food just by mercy, but treat him as your *deva*; have some *māhātmya*. So that's why all the — whatever *ṣoḍaśopacāra pūjana* is there, there was *atithi* also; it was there: you have to not only feed him, just like you are doing some favour to him, but you have to wash your — wash his feet, give some *āsana*, give some *vastra*; like that you have to do his *pūjana*. You must feel that he is of higher level than you, is of *deva* level, and then before you — so you have to worship him. That was — you have to do *pūjā*. So that was there. # Brief Summary

**Content.** The second session continues the Subodhinī on Kardama's consolation of Devahūti (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.24.3–6) and reaches his words to the new-born Kapila (3.24.30–31). The governing question is the relation of *sādhana* and *phala* when the fruit — Bhagavān himself — has already been promised: why do the means at all, when one does not reopen the books once the exam is over? Following Mahāprabhuji, the speaker answers that in Puṣṭi Bhakti-mārga the *sādhanas* are not causes but responses: they make Bhagavān known (*bodhayanti*), make him available (*prāpnuvanti*), bring him under the sweet control of love (*vaśīkurvanti*), and even produce his *bhāvātmaka* form (*utpādayanti*). Bhagavān comes *svayam eva*, not as a *pratīka*; he has jumped into the well himself, so the *sādhanas* are not for *ātmoddhāra* but are the wedding gift of a heart that appreciates his coming — and only a host who likes to be fed can be feasted. Since Īśvara is controlled by nothing, the means are to be done "as if nothing were accomplished" (*asiddhavad eva*), with *śraddhā*, which is why one who acts from love always finds what he has done too little. The five *sādhanas* of the verse — *dama*, *niyama*, *tapa*, *draviṇa*, *dāna* — are expounded, with the Puṣṭimārgī alternative to the *bhogī* and the *tyāgī*: enjoy, but with Bhagavān and as his *prasāda*. The fourth verse yields the *nirdoṣa-guṇapūrṇa* son who spreads Kardama's glory, cuts the knot of the heart and gives *brahmānubhava*. After questions on *aprākṛta guṇa* and on the three debts — answered with the thesis that Puṣṭimārga knows whole and part, not *ṛṇa*, *bhakti* being the fifth *puruṣārtha* — the lecture turns to Devahūti's worship and Bhagavān's birth like fire from wood, to the *pramāṇa*-status of Bhagavān's word as equal to the Veda, and to the forms he takes for his *bhaktas*, all of one material, Brahman, like things of gold. It closes with Mahāprabhuji's three answers to "where is *bhakti* in the Veda?": the forms are recent (*idānīntanatvāt*), the Veda neither limits nor forbids them (*etāvattvaṁ niṣedhaś ca nāsty eva*), and their seeming lowliness is a defect of *buddhi* (*anyathā-bhānaṁ buddhi-doṣāt*) which only *bhakti* overcomes.
