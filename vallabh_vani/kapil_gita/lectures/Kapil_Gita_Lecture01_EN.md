# Brief Summary

**Content.** The opening session frames the Kapila Gītā before its first verse is read. Vyāsa divides the Veda, composes the Mahābhārata and the Brahmasūtra, and still feels an emptiness until Nārada tells him he has not sung the glory of Śrī Kṛṣṇa; the Bhāgavata is then revealed to him in *samādhi*. Purāṇa makes the Veda's abstract teaching concrete (Prahlāda and the pillar), and the dry Upaniṣadic definition of Brahman (Taittirīya 3.1.1, Brahmasūtra 1.1.2) becomes in the Bhāgavata the tenfold *līlā* from *sarga* to *āśraya*, relished through *śravaṇa*, *kīrtana* and *smaraṇa* rather than the *jñāna-mārga*'s *śravaṇa*, *manana* and *nididhyāsana*. The *adhikārī* is the *rasika bhakta* who has *śraddhā* that the Bhāgavata is *śāstra*; Mahāprabhuji's mission to produce the Bhāgavatārtha and his *saptārtha* (*śāstra*, *skandha*, *prakaraṇa*, *adhyāya*, *vākya*, *pada*, *akṣara*), the Tattvārthadīpanibandha and the Subodhinī are introduced. The three selected chapters of the Kapila Gītā are shown to be the foundation of the Puṣṭimārga's *bhakti*, *sevā*, *sevā-phala*, epistemology, hermeneutics and metaphysics; Sāṅkhya's analytic method is set against Vedānta's synthetic method, and *sāṅkhya* and *yoga* are the *kuṇḍalas* beside the face of *bhakti*. Adhyāya 24 begins: Kapila is Hari, and Kardama pays the three debts to *ṛṣis*, *devas* and *pitṛs* before *sannyāsa*, which opens a long excursus on indebtedness as the driving force of society, ecology as *yajña*, and the *gṛhastha*'s entitlement to enjoy. Devahūti's *nirveda* is the pendulum swinging from *bhoga* to self-abuse; Kardama consoles her (3.24.1–4): the Akṣara will be born from her womb, so that the creator is created by his parents — the proof that the Bhāgavata is *līlā* and not a machine, since *mukti* appears already in the third skandha. The closing exchange defines Īśvara as governing *sattā* and insists that *ātmanindā* and *doṣa-darśana*, useful perhaps for *vairāgya*, have no place in *bhakti*.

**Examples and Stories.** The thread that binds scattered flowers into a *mālā* (Brahmasūtra); Vyāsa's emptiness and Nārada's counsel; Kṛṣṇa as a side character of the Mahābhārata; Prahlāda, Hiraṇyakaśipu, the pillar and Nṛsiṁha; Kṛṣṇa dancing before the Vraja-bhaktas for butter; the wave on the ocean of *ānanda*; singing at every good occasion in India; the picnic tourist who takes selfies and enjoys the food more than the place; twelfth-pass eligibility for admission to Vallabha Vedānta, and admission without study bearing no fruit; European masters calling the Purāṇas myth; the child frightened or delighted by a story; a movie's dialogue, action, story and theme; the heap of sand against the organic growth of a plant (*vṛddhi* and *pravṛddhi*); knowing gold to know all golden ornaments; the revolution in chemistry, electrons, protons and the classification table; engineering super-colleges teaching specialists; the drop of blood analysed in a laboratory; the king residing in the city of the body; ornaments beautified by Bhagavān, not Bhagavān by ornaments, and wealthy *svarūpas*; "give to needy" against paying a debt; becoming *divāḷā* if debts are not paid; Kapila and Patañjali as founders of Sāṅkhya and Yoga; the three-year course of Vallabha Vedānta from Ṣoḍaśa Grantha to Aṇubhāṣya; earning in society, not in a forest; a trillion galaxies and the solar system less than a dot; Indologists calling river-worshippers pagans and jungle people; polluted rivers and the West now teaching ecology; the Gujarati renunciation talk "koī koī nu nathī re"; the pendulum from *bhoga* to self-blame; amoeba, microbe and virus possessing consciousness while a broken stone does not respond; the machine against the waves; Adam's sin and fallen creatures against *mātā pṛthivī*; Dayārām's *pada* "prakaṭ maḷe sukh thāy" and licking the earth for water; a doubtful destination disturbing a journey; Yogeśvara and Nareśvara; abusing a painting abuses the painter; *asuras* who fight the Lord receive *mukti*.
