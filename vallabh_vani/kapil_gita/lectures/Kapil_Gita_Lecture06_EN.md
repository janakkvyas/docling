# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session reaches the definition (*lakṣaṇa*) of *bhakti* in Kapila's teaching, Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.25.32–33, and reads it word by word with Mahāprabhuji's Subodhinī. *Devānām* is taken as *devī indriyāṇām*: the organs, motor and sensory, are of two kinds by their basic orientation — *devarūpa*, inclined to the *alaukika* work and knowledge taught by the Veda, and *āsurarūpa*, inclined to *laukika* things only — on the authority of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka's *dvayā prājāpatyāḥ*. The *āsurī* organs are always the stronger, so the *devī* organs cannot do their work until, by *āsanopāsanā*, *prāṇāyāma* and *prāṇopāsanā*, the organs are freed of the *asaṅgādi doṣa* and become *devī* in action too; this is a long process, made natural for ṛṣis and devas by the practice of many births, and offered here to Devahūti, who is neither. The speaker insists there is no total division of mankind into godly and satanic: both orientations sit in the same organ, and the only conquest the system knows is *ātmajaya*. *Guṇaliṅgānām* is explained through its two compound break-ups — the organs are known only by their working — and the *devī* organs are *laya-vikṣepa-śūnya*: neither sunk in themselves nor wavering, doing their work so smoothly that they do not show their presence. *Ānuśravika-karmaṇām* names the organs whose work is Vedic while their involvement in the world is a mere looking-on; by *daiva*, by great fortune, such organs, after engaging in *karma*, yoga and *jñāna*, come at the *phalāvasthā* to rest by nature in the *śuddha-sattva* form of Bhagavān, *sattva* being said from the Sāṅkhya angle while the Bhāgavata itself calls everything dedicated to Him *nirguṇa*. *Ekamanasaḥ* extends the twofold analysis to the mind: the *āsura* mind is *saṅkalpa-vikalpa* and shaken by the *guṇas*, the *daiva* mind is of one nature and reflective (*manana*); the organs may remain of both kinds so long as the *āsurī* work is checked, but the mind can have no second object — bhakti done for business or any other purpose ceases to be bhakti. For beginners the Subodhinī allows a partial *vṛtti* which, being *akṣaya*, accumulates over many births into the natural *vṛtti* of the last birth, as in Prahlāda; hence the qualifiers *animittā* (the red rose that excludes every other rose) and *bhāgavatī* (having Bhagavān alone as its object, or taking on His six *guṇas* as *Hari-āveśa*). The Pañcāgnividyā frame, rain, food, body and seed as successive oblations, introduces the four stages of the *brahmavid* and the two *muktis*, *krama* and *sadyaḥ*; Mahāprabhuji's *siddhānta* is that lifting oneself above the world while living in it belongs in fact to *bhaktas* alone, and that this bhakti is weightier than the *siddhi* of *mukti*, since it quickly wears out the body's *kośa* as the stomach-fire alone digests what is swallowed — the organs themselves being assimilated into bhakti and becoming a *bhakta-deha*, the instrument of Bhagavān.
