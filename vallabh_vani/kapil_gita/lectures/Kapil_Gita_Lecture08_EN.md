And bud nature of the flower, in bud of the flower, is the same as opening of the flower; but both the *avasthā*, both the nature, is different: it is concealed — the fragrance, the beauty is concealed in the bud nature — and it is very permanent and open in the blossoming flower. So in this case Mahāprabhuji refers to *sevāyāṁ kriyamāṇāyāṁ phalaṁ bhavati* ‹श्रवणाधारित› — while doing *sevā* this *phala-avasthā* is reached. But here, the beginning only — in Navaratna also we have said that *bhagavān api puṣṭisthaḥ* — in the beginning only Bhagavān, or the *phala* — who is the *phala*? — is the giver of the *phala*, *phala-dātā*; *phala* Himself is accompanying us. But due to our *saṁsāritā*, *ahantā-mamatā*, which is not totally — that in graph also we have seen — *ahantā-mamatā* is totally not encompassed by *bhakti*; we feel the other *ahantā-mamatā* which does not belong to *bhakti*, means our *saṁsāra* remains and our *bhakti* also. But after, in *phala-avasthā*, what happens: the total of our *saṁsāra* starts covering up by the *bhakti*. So in that case the *sādhana* itself becomes *phala*.

# 3. The Three Sevā-phalas and the Madhyamā Bhakti Verses (3.25.37–38): Kāla Has No Control over Sevā

*[00:12:40]*

**Speaker:** And another aspect also we have learned: first is *alaukika sāmarthya*. By living only in our body, in our body parts, in our sense and motor organs, such a capability comes that it transcends the level of *laukikatā* and they acquire *alaukika sāmarthya*. That is discussed in nature of *bhakti* also: that that type of competition ensues in our body parts, that "let me go first to serve Śrī Kṛṣṇa" — *aham-ahamikayā*. So that type of *alaukika sāmarthya* is the first. Then second is the *sāyujya*, which is going to be discussed here — and then, which I think we have discussed already — and then third is *sevopayogī deho vaikuṇṭhādiṣu*: you are not able to enjoy the *phala-avasthā* in this body; after leaving this body you get placed amongst the devotees in Vaikuṇṭhādi *loka* — Vraja‹?›, Vaikuṇṭhādi, which is *ādi* — you get stay in the *bhaktas*, in abode of Bhagavān; you get the place where you can serve; you start serving Him again as a *sevaka*. So this is *sevā-phala*: *sevopayogī deho vaikuṇṭhādiṣu* — *sevā-upayogī deha*, *vaikuṇṭhādiṣu*. So it is said:

> अथो विभूतिं मम मायाविनस्ताम्  
> ऐश्वर्यमष्टाङ्गमनुप्रवृत्तम् ।  
> श्रियं भागवतीं वास्पृहयन्ति भद्रां  
> परस्य मे तेऽश्नुवते तु लोके ॥  
>
> *atho vibhūtiṁ mama māyāvinas tām*  
> *aiśvaryam aṣṭāṅgam anupravṛttam |*  
> *śriyaṁ bhāgavatīṁ vāspṛhayanti bhadrāṁ*  
> *parasya me te 'śnuvate tu loke ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.25.37)
>
> [Māyā is always not the one which fools you; capability, superpower, also is known as *māyā*. In that also the *aṣṭāṅga aiśvarya*, the *aṣṭa-siddhi*, and the *bhāgavatī śrī*, the Śrī of Bhagavān — they do not have *spṛhā*; they do not long for it; that type of *bhāgavatī śrī* also they don't want; and then they become devoted to Bhagavān in *bhagavat-loka*.]

This — *mama māyāvinaḥ*: "I am *māyāvī*" — Māyā is always not the one which fools you, but Māyā is also capability, superpower; capability also is known as *māyā*. *Mama māyāvino vibhūtim. Tām aiśvaryam aṣṭāṅgam anupravṛttam* — in that also, *aṣṭāṅgam aiśvaryam*: *aiśvaryādi*, *aṣṭa-siddhi*. *Śriyaṁ bhāgavatīm*: *bhāgavatī siddhi*, *māne* [means] *śreyas-sādhikā*, Śrī of Bhagavān. They do not — *aspṛhayanti*: they do not have *spṛhā*; they do not long for it; they do not want it. They do not want — *aspṛhayanti bhadrām*: *bhadram*, *kalyāṇakārī*; *bhāgavatīṁ śriyam api te na vāñchanti* — that type of *bhāgavatī śrī* also they don't want. *Parasya me te 'śnuvate tu loke*: and then that they become devoted to Bhagavān in *loka*, *bhagavat-loka*.

> न कर्हिचिन्मत्पराः शान्तरूपे  
> नङ्क्ष्यन्ति नो मेऽनिमिषो लेढि हेतिः ।  
> येषामहं प्रिय आत्मा सुतश्च  
> सखा गुरुः सुहृदो दैवमिष्टम् ॥  
>
> *na karhicin mat-parāḥ śānta-rūpe*  
> *naṅkṣyanti no me 'nimiṣo leḍhi hetiḥ |*  
> *yeṣām ahaṁ priya ātmā sutaś ca*  
> *sakhā guruḥ suhṛdo daivam iṣṭam ||* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.25.38)
>
> [Those who are associated with me, *mat-parāḥ*, never get destroyed in Vaikuṇṭha, the *śānta-rūpa*; the *kālacakra*, the *heti* of Kāla, does not lick them away, does not eat them up — because for them I myself become eightfold: the *priya*, the *ātmā*, the *suta*, the *sakhā*, the *guru*, the *suhṛd* and the *iṣṭa daiva*.]

There, in Sevā-phala, Mahāprabhuji said that in this *sevā*, pertaining to *sevā*, whatever *phala* is there and *adhikāra* is there — *na kālādi-niyāmakatā*: *kālādi* cannot control them. Because Kāla is the main controller in this *sṛṣṭi*. When Kāla is produced, under the Kāla everything is produced; whatever is produced gets produced in Kāla, sustains itself in Kāla, and also submerges in Kāla, due to Kāla. So Kāla is *mukhya adhikārī* of Bhagavān, and its power — power of Kāla is there over everything whichever is *mahat*. But here, in case of *sevā-phala* and *sevā-adhikāra*, there is no *kāla-niyāmakatā*; Kāla cannot control it, do not have any control over either — *adhikāra*: *adhikāra* is based on *varaṇa* only; and *phala* also is depend on *bhagavad-phalamatā-upapatte*‹?› — given by Bhagavān only.

I don't want the kingdom of heaven; I do not want the level of getting the level of Brahmājī — *na nāka-pṛṣṭhaṁ na ca pārameṣṭhyaṁ na sārva-bhaumam*: on the earth I do not want to be *cakravartī* of the whole earth; *na nāka-pṛṣṭhaṁ na ca pārameṣṭhyaṁ na sārva-bhaumaṁ na rasādhipatyam* — I do not want to be the king of *pātāla-loka* also; *samañjasa* — and anything I do not want; I want You only. # 5. Sudāmā, Arjuna and Duryodhana: the Māyāvī Who Cheats by Giving

*[00:23:09]*

**Speaker:** And the beautiful example is there in case of Sudāmā also: when he went there to meet Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa, and Śrī Kṛṣṇa did not give him anything — nothing, not a single. So he, while returning — he was so *bhakta* — they thought that: why He did not give me anything? Because He thought that I will be engrossed in this; if I get wealth, I will be engrossed:

> अधनोऽयं धनं प्राप्य माद्यन्नुच्चैर्न मां स्मरेत् ।  
>
> *adhano 'yaṁ dhanaṁ prāpya mādyann uccair na māṁ smaret |* (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 10.81.20)
>
> [If I give him wealth, he will be so much engrossed, and so much become proud of it, and so much engrossed in wealth that he will forget me.]

If you — why He did not give me wealth? He loves me; that's why He has not given me wealth. By giving me wealth — He may have thought that if I give him wealth, he will be so much engrossed, and so much become proud of it, and so much engrossed in wealth that he will forget me. So he knows that this is *vibhūti* of Bhagavān.

So in case of Arjuna and Duryodhana also, when they went to beg Bhagavān to come to their side in Mahābhārata *yuddha*: Arjuna — the Duryodhana asked for the *senā* of Bhagavān, Nārāyaṇī *senā*, and Arjuna asked for Bhagavān Himself. And that Bhagavān also said: I'll accompany the one, but I'll not take any *astra* or *śastra*, anything; I will be alone, without any weapon; I will not wear any of my weapon, and I will be all alone, with that, without my all the power. But Duryodhana preferred the *senā*, or power, of Bhagavān; Arjuna preferred Bhagavān only. So it is said *māyāvī* — that way it is *māyāvī*; not like that everything is *mithyā*, not like that. He is *māyāvī*: He cheats us by not coming to us, but by giving all these things. And so *putra-dhanādi-rūpāṁ svargādi-rūpāṁ ca na tatra bhogyam asti iti māyāvina iti uktam*.

One may, in this world — one may aspire for all these things; but — we will aspire for, due to our own deeds, due to our own hard work, and anything we want, many things. Just like Vṛtrāsura also wanted to kill Indra and acquire the kingdom of *svarga*; that's why he was fighting fiercely. It's not like that he did not want anything. He is not like that of *vairāgī*, *jñānamārgī vairāgī*, that "I don't want anything, everything is *mithyā*" — not like that. He wanted to conquer the heaven, because he was born for that, to fight with Indra; so he wanted to kill Indra, or capture the heaven. He wanted that; but he did not want from Bhagavān, or by doing *bhakti*; he did not want anything. He wanted — that was his want. Same way *bhakta* may want anything; but they do not want — and here in *madhyama bhakti* they do not want — *putra-dhanādi-rūpāṁ svargādi-rūpāṁ ca na tatra bhogyam asti*: there they feel, what is there to enjoy it? When — if you go to higher level, if you go to higher level, you will not enjoy the lower-level things. So what is — *na tatra bhogyam asti iti māyāvina iti uktam*.

# 6. Why the Ṣaṣṭhī in *māyāvinaḥ*: the Kārakas and Bhagavad-adhīnatva

# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session closes the Bhakti-yoga chapter of the Kapila Gītā (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.25.37–44) and reads it alongside Vallabhācārya's Sevā-phala. The speaker first restates the sādhana–phala relation peculiar to sevā — the fruit arises within the doing (*sevāyāṁ kriyamāṇāyāṁ phalam*), as the flower is already in the bud — and corrects a misreading of the vārtā of Āskaraṇ Dāsjī: reaching mānasī sevā does not end tanu-vittajā sevā except in the rare vihvala state in which one cannot even care for one's body. He then recalls the three sevā-phalas (alaukika sāmarthya, sāyujya, sevopayogī deha in Vaikuṇṭha) and takes up verses 37–38 as the fruit of madhyamā bhakti, working through the Subodhinī phrase by phrase: the force of *atha*; the genitive *māyāvinaḥ*, Bhagavān being māyāvī because He cheats by giving vibhūti rather than Himself (Sudāmā, Arjuna and Duryodhana); the eight siddhis and the Indian ideal of controlling oneself rather than nature; śrī as all sampatti up to mokṣa; the dismissive *vā*; and the objection *kṣīṇe puṇye martya-lokaṁ viśanti*, answered by the śānta-rūpa Vaikuṇṭha beyond Kāla, where the Lord Himself becomes the eight viṣayas through which Kāla acts — viṣaya, deha, putra, mitra, guru, bāndhava, suhṛd and deva. A student's question on the four muktis draws out the maryādā–Puṣṭi distinction: the Puṣṭi bhakta desires no mukti, bhakti is the fifth puruṣārtha, Bhagavān may yet grant mukti, and the mukta too can do bhakti (*muktopasṛpya-vyapadeśāt*), though alaukika sāmarthya — enjoying the immortal Lord with these mortal, jaḍa indriyas — remains the higher phala; Puṣṭi bhakti, imparted by varaṇa, is wider than the sampradāya. The lecture ends with verses 39–44 — ananya bhakti beyond mṛtyu, fear removed only by the Īśvara of pradhāna and puruṣa, jñāna and vairāgya as children of bhakti, *mano mayy arpitaṁ sthiram* — and a reflection on tanmayatā, the wandering mind finding its full range in the ever-new līlā, before the three phalas are summed up as *sevāyāṁ phalatrayam*: fruit in sevā, not of sevā. Next session: the Sāṅkhya, "metaphysics of Vallabhācārya".
