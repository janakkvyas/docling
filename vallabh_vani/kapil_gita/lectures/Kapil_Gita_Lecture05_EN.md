# Brief Summary

**Content.** The session opens by reviewing Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.25.20: *prasaṅga*, long emotional company, is the unageing noose of the *ātmā*, because what we are rests on *ahaṅkāra* (who am I) and *māyā* (what is mine), and what is mine depends on *saṅga* alone. A question from the floor on whether *prasaṅga* also means *saṅghāta* leads the speaker to distinguish the two: the body is an aggregate of separate, replaceable parts which *ahantā* binds into "I", as a rope binds sticks into one bundle, whereas *prasaṅga* is the slow growth of *mamatā* by association, as a new house or a bride's in-laws' home become "mine" only with time. The same *prasaṅga* directed to the *satpuruṣa* is the open door of liberation, so Kapila describes the *sādhu-puruṣa* (3.25.21–24), whom the speaker sorts, with the help of a chart he had circulated, into sixteen qualities: two general (*sarva-saṅga-vivarjita*, *saṅga-doṣa-hara*), three *ādhibhautika*, four *ādhyātmika* and seven *ādhidaivika*, the last concerning *dṛḍha bhakti*, leaving works and relatives for the Lord's sake, taking shelter in him, and hearing and telling his *kathā* in a cycle that must not split into professional *vaktā* and passive *śrotā*. Through the Subodhinī Kārikā he argues that every *bhakta* faces difficulties until *bhakti* ripens into *rasa*, that bearing them is itself *tapa*, that such naturally arriving difficulties please Bhagavān more than mere *kriyā*, and that *bhakti* which cannot climb to *rasa* falls back to the level of *dharma*. Subodhinī's definition of *bhakti* — all the organs, together with the mind, vying to be first in the Lord's work — is unpacked, as is *ananya-bhāva*, with its three negations (*devatāntara*, *padārthāntara*, *mārgāntara*) and the four obstacles (*pramāṇa*, *prameya*, *sādhana*, *phala*) whose absence makes *bhakti* *dṛḍhā*. The *saṅga-doṣa-hara* quality is then explained as not making the seeker dependent: the true *bhagavadīya*, like Śukadevjī with Parīkṣit, attaches you to Bhagavān, not to himself. Verse 3.25.25 is read as *prakṛṣṭa*, reciprocal *satsaṅga* in which only Bhagavān's glory is sung, producing in turn *śraddhā*, *rati* and *bhakti*; 3.25.26–27 describe *vairāgya* and *jñāna* arising as the sons of *bhakti*, the seeing of the creator in the creation, the gentle *ṛju yoga-mārga* by which *bhakti* seeps into the *citta*, and the attainment of the Lord as the innermost self. Devahūti's three questions (3.25.28–30) and Maitreya's bridge verse (3.25.31) set up the next session's definition of *bhakti*. The closing discussion, prompted by two students, turns on paid religious service: in Puṣṭimārga *bhakti* can never be instrumental to duty, earning, desire or liberation, a habit of professional hearing and singing leads only to more of the same, what is good or bad for *bhakti* is to be learnt from Mahāprabhuji and not from the crowd, and justification must flow from the guru to us rather than the reverse.
