**Content.** The shortest session of the series, delivered over a failing internet connection, opens with a recapitulation: the enumeration (*uddeśa*) of the fundamental *tattvas* is complete, *kāla* has been treated as the fifth form of Akṣara Brahma and as the *upalakṣaṇa* of Bhagavān, who acts from outside as time and from inside as *puruṣa*. Because the senses flow outward, the inner instrument is never an object of perception (*alpaṁ hi pratyakṣam*); the outward-turned are devoured by *kāla*, while the one who is *svastha*, settled in his own self, is nourished by the *puruṣa* — the speaker reads this through the Āyurvedic distinction between being merely disease-free and being *svastha*, and through Mahāprabhuji's Śikṣāśloka warning that the body and *citta* in the stream of time will devour the *bahirmukha*; hence *bhakti* must be purposeless and preceded by *śaraṇāgati*. Turning to Śrīmad Bhāgavata 3.26.19–21 with the Subodhinī, the speaker explains the method of defining each *tattva* four ways — by its origination and by its *ādhyātmika*, *ādhidaivika* and *ādhibhautika* aspects — and expands at length on Mahāprabhuji's three strata of everything (gross, systemic, presiding deity), which he holds to be the reason Sanātana Dharma survived iconoclasm and conversion while other cultures did not, and which he fears is now being surrendered from within. The *utpatti* of Mahat is then read as the conception of a cosmic embryo: *prakṛti*, stirred from equilibrium by time, is the ovum; the *puruṣa* supplies the seed of *sat* and *cit*; the first interaction of the inert and the conscious is Mahat, golden-bodied like heated iron. Its *ādhyātmika* definition makes it the *jagad-aṅkura*, the sprout holding the encoded universe, which by its own light drinks the primordial darkness that the speaker likens to a black hole, and which possesses three greatnesses — primordial receptivity, primordial cause, primordial illumination. Its *ādhidaivika* form is Vāsudeva, *viśuddha sattva*, the *sattva* of *sattva*, whose three qualities *svaccha*, *śānta* and *bhagavataḥ padam* correspond to *sat*, *cit* and *ānanda*; the *citta* is therefore the seat of *upāsanā*, Vāsudeva's body and support, and the lecture closes with the Subodhinī's resolution that the origination of the individual *citta* is already given in that of Mahat, the *samaṣṭi* of which the *citta* is the *vyaṣṭi*.
