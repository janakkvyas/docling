# English translation books of a stuti with the Subodhinī — brief and house style

A book of this kind gives, verse by verse, a passage of the Śrīmad Bhāgavata (here a stuti, a hymn of praise inside a chapter) with Śrī Mahāprabhuji's Subodhinī on it: the Sanskrit in Devanagari, its IAST transliteration, and an English translation that follows Gosvāmī Śrī Śyām Manoharjī's explanation of the text in his lectures. A student of his translates; others verify the verses and do the typesetting.

The brief was learned from the exemplar the owner shared on 2026-10-23: **॥ नलकूबरमणिग्रीवकृतस्तुति ॥ / ॥ Nalakūbaramaṇigrīvakṛtastuti ॥**, revision 1, translated by Sital Thakrar (Śrīmad Bhāgavata 10.10.29–38, the prayer of Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva, with the Subodhinī), set in Adobe InDesign 20.5 and exported to PDF on 2026-02-01: 59 PDF pages of 5.5 × 8.5 in. Page references below are the book's own page numbers (front matter i–iii, then 1–55; PDF page = book page + 4). Where the exemplar is inconsistent, the rule here is the one to follow; the inconsistencies are listed at the end so the owner can decide otherwise.

## What the book is for, and the rules that follow from it

- **The verses and the Subodhinī are given as printed in the edition the translator works from**, never retyped from memory or from another edition. The editors verify each verse against the print (the preface thanks two people for "verifying the verses"). A reading that differs from the common Gita Press text stays when it is the Subodhinī's reading (e.g. ३६ *namaḥ paramakalyāṇa ! namaste viśvamaṃgala !*).
- **The translation follows the speaker's explanation.** The book is a student's rendering of Gosvāmījī's (Āpaśrī's) lectures on the passage, so where the Subodhinī is terse, the English unfolds it the way he explains it; where it adds explanation, it says so in its own sentence rather than altering the rendering. Nothing is invented: no doctrine, no story, no citation that the Subodhinī or the lectures do not give.
- **Sanskrit terms stay visible.** The English names a concept and gives the term in brackets, in roman type (not italics), in IAST: "the greatness (māhātmya)", "incomprehensibility (durjñeya)", "the inner controller (antaryāmī)". Tradition-internal names and titles are kept: Bhagavān, Śrīkṛṣṇa, Śrīmahāprabhujī, Āpaśrī, Puruṣottama, Vāsudeva.
- **Register**: formal, devotional English; God, Bhagavān and the pronouns He/His/Him capitalised; British spelling (practise, emphasises, fulfils); long sentences are allowed, but each paragraph makes one step of the commentary's argument.
- Sources of quotations in the commentary keep the Subodhinī's own form in the Devanagari and IAST, and a consistent English form in the translation (see *Citations*).

## The book, part by part (exemplar pages)

1. **Title page** (no folio): the work's name in Devanagari between double daṇḍas, `॥ नलकूबरमणिग्रीवकृतस्तुति ॥` (21 pt), below it the IAST between double daṇḍas (16 pt); much lower, the speaker's name in Devanagari, `गोस्वामी श्याम मनोहर` (18 pt), and in IAST, `Goswāmy Śyāma Manohara` (15 pt). Everything centred. The speaker is named as the author; the translator signs the preface.
2. **Preface** (pp. i–ii), heading `Preface` (16 pt, centred): the translator, in the first person, on how the book came about, whose lectures it rests on, and thanks by name to those who edited, verified and formatted it; signed at the end, right-aligned, in 14 pt (`Sital Thakrar`).
3. **Contents** (p. iii), heading `Content :` (12 pt, centred, as printed; read *Contents*): a three-column table, `S. No.` | `Subject` | `P.No.`, one row per part, the subject given in Devanagari (11 pt) with its IAST below (10 pt), the page number in the right column (12 pt). Front-matter folios are lower-case roman (i, ii, iii); the text starts again at 1.
4. **Summary of verses** (pp. 1–7), heading 14 pt centred, then, centred, one line each: `॥ श्रीमद्भागवतदशमसुबोधिनी ॥` and its IAST, `॥ नलकूबरमणिग्रीवकृतस्तुति ॥` and its IAST, the chapter's place in the Subodhinī's scheme in brackets (`(निरोधलीलान्तर्गत तामसप्रकरणके अवान्तर प्रमाणप्रकरणमें वैराग्यगुणलीलापरक दसवां अध्याय)`) with its IAST and a one-sentence English rendering; then the heading of the chapter-summary block (`(भगवत्स्वरूप विवेचनपरक)`, `( अध्यायार्थ )`, `( सुबोधिनीकारिकाः )`, each with IAST); then **the Subodhinī's summary kārikās** for the chapter, each as two centred Devanagari lines (12 pt) followed by two centred IAST lines (11 pt), numbered `॥ १ ॥` / `॥ 1 ॥`; then an English introduction and a `Synopsis:` (13 pt, indented like a paragraph) whose paragraphs paraphrase the kārikās in order, each ending with the kārikā's number, `॥ 1 ॥` … `॥11॥`.
5. **One part per verse** (pp. 8–53), not starting on a new page; about 48 pt of space before each part. Each part has, in this order:
   - the part's title: Devanagari (Hindi) in round brackets, 14 pt centred, e.g. `(कृष्णकी सर्वरूपता)`; its IAST in brackets, 13 pt centred, `(kṛṣṇakī sarvarūpatā)`; from the seventh part on also an English title line, 11–12 pt centred (`Due to His self-manifested qualities, God remains unmanifest.`);
   - the label `श्लोक :` (12 pt, centred) and the verse in Devanagari, centred, one half-verse (pāda pair) per line, 12 pt on a 24.3 pt line; a speaker line first where the text has one (`॥ नलकूबरमणिग्रीवौ उचतुः ॥`); the verse number of the Bhāgavata chapter at the end (`||२९||`);
   - the label `śloka:` and the IAST, centred, 11 pt, same line breaks, Arabic number (`||29||`);
   - the **translation of the verse**: one or two justified paragraphs (12 pt), sometimes opening with `Translation:`;
   - the label `सुबोधिनी :` (12 pt, indented like a paragraph) and the **Subodhinī in Devanagari**, justified paragraphs with first-line indent;
   - the label `Subodhinī:` and the **Subodhinī in IAST**, the same paragraphs transliterated, justified, hyphenated;
   - `Translation:` and the **English translation of the Subodhinī**, several paragraphs, unfolding it step by step.
   A connecting question may stand between two parts in three short lines, Devanagari, IAST and English, indented (p. 28: `ऐसी स्थितिमें छुटकारा कैसे होगा ?` / `aisī sthitimeṃ chuṭakārā kaise hogā ?` / `How can one attain liberation from such a state?`).
6. The book ends after the last part (p. 53); pp. 54–55 are blank to complete the printed signature.

`tools/make_ref_booklet.py english` writes a Word template with these values ("Sanskrit Text" named as the font: it is installed with Windows). Our builds substitute Noto Serif and Noto Serif Devanagari, the closest free fonts, whose Latin is about 5 % wider, so a build runs a little longer than the original (the two-page preface takes three). The exemplar itself was set in InDesign; the template is for drafts and for editors who work in Word.

## Conventions of the text

- **Transliteration**: IAST with `ṃ` for anusvāra (`idaṃ`, `vahāṃ`), `ḥ` for visarga, `’` for avagraha (`sarvarūpo’pi`); Hindi titles are transliterated letter for letter without dropping the inherent vowel (`kṛṣṇakī sarvarūpatā`, `bhagavān aprakaṭa`, `jahāṃ bhī hama jāyeṃ`). The IAST of a verse follows the Devanagari line for line and keeps its sandhi and word division.
- **Numbers**: Devanagari digits in Devanagari lines, Arabic digits in IAST and English lines; verse numbers are the Bhāgavata chapter's (29–38 for 10.10.29–38), kārikā numbers the Subodhinī's (1–11).
- **Daṇḍas**: `॥` closes each half of a verse line pair; one form for the number, `॥२९॥` (the exemplar mixes `||२९||`, `॥ ३४ ॥`, `|| ३६ |`).
- **Labels** have one spelling each; the exemplar has `श्लोक :`, `श्लोकः`, `श्लोक:`, `śloka:`, `ślokaḥ :`, `सुबोधिनी :`, `सुबोधिनि:`, `Subodhinī:`, `Subodhinīḥ :`. House form: `श्लोक:` / `śloka:` / `सुबोधिनी:` / `Subodhinī:`, the colon directly after the word (the commonest IAST forms of the exemplar).
- **Citations**: inside the Devanagari and IAST Subodhinī, as the edition prints them (`(भाग।पुरा।११।१९।३)`); in English, one form throughout: `(Bhāgavata Purāṇa 11.19.3)`, `(Bhagavadgītā 11.40)`, `(Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.8)`, `(Kena Upaniṣad 2.3)`, `(Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.12.55)`. The exemplar writes the same reference several ways and twice lost the dot between chapter and verse (`śvetā.upa. 318` for 3.8, `kenopa. 213`).
- **Names**: one spelling each, with diacritics: Nalakūbara (not Nalakūbera), Maṇigrīva, Nārada, Śrīkṛṣṇa, Mahāyogī, Gosvāmī Śrī Śyām Manoharjī in running English (the title page's `Goswāmy Śyāma Manohara` is the form the owner chose for the title; keep it there unless the owner changes it).
- **The English** keeps one term per concept across the book (e.g. *ādhidaivika* "divine", *ādhyātmika* "spiritual", *ādhibhautika* "material"), and glosses a term once per part.

## Workflow for a new book of this kind

1. **Fix the text.** Get the Devanagari of the verses and of the Subodhinī from the printed edition the translator uses (scan or PDF), transcribe it exactly, and have it verified against the print (two readers). Number the verses as the Bhāgavata chapter does.
2. **Transliterate** each Devanagari line to IAST (`tools/iast.py` gives the house transliteration; check every line by eye, especially sandhi, avagraha and anusvāra).
3. **Gather the explanation**: the speaker's lectures on the passage, transcribed with the Sarvam pipeline (`vallabh_vani/tools/`), translated where they are in Hindi; mark in them where each verse and each sentence of the Subodhinī is explained.
4. **Translate**: verse first (close, readable, with the key terms in brackets), then the Subodhinī paragraph by paragraph, following the lectures' explanation; one paragraph of English per step of the commentary. Keep a running list of the term renderings.
5. **Assemble** in the markdown above, write the summary part (the Subodhinī's adhyāyārtha kārikās, then the synopsis paragraph for each), the preface (by the translator) and the contents.
6. **Check** with `lint_booklet.py` (labels, numbering, duplicated paragraphs, spelling of names), read the whole book once against the print for the Sanskrit, and once for the English alone.
7. **Build** the Word file with the template (`build_booklet.py`) and deliver it: the owner receives Word files only, never the PDF or the markdown.

## Where the exemplar is inconsistent with itself (for the owner's decision)

Counted in the clean copy of the exemplar with `tools/lint_booklet.py`:

- **Labels**: `श्लोक:` 6, `श्लोक :` 3; `śloka:` 7, `śloka :` 1, `ślokaḥ :` 1; `सुबोधिनी :` 5, `सुबोधिनी:` 2, `सुबोधिनि:` 2, `सुबोधिनि :` 1; `Subodhinī:` 7, `Subodhinīh :` 2, `Subodhinīḥ :` 1; and once `श्लोक :-२, मूलश्लोक - ३०` / `śloka:- 2, mūlaśloka:- 30 )` (p. 14). `Content :` for *Contents*; `S. N0.` with a zero.
- **Verse-number marks**: `॥N॥` 44 times, `|N|` 24, `||N||` 6, `||N|` 2, `।।N।।` once.
- **The visarga typed as a colon** in Devanagari verse lines (`दशभि:`, `निर्णय:`, `गृह्यमाणै:`, `रज:`), and the IAST typed separately from the Devanagari, so that the two sometimes disagree (`prāṇabhṛcchralokaiḥ` for प्राणभृच्छ्लोकैः, `jagad rūpas` for जगद्रुपस्). Transliterating the checked Devanagari with `tools/iast.py` keeps the two in step: it reproduces the printed IAST in 40 of the 45 verse lines, and the other five are these slips.
- `Tranlsation:` on p. 20.
- **Contents against headings**: the contents prints `भक्तिकी स्थापनाके लिए भगवत्प्राकट्य होता है` where the heading on p. 37 has `स्थापनके` (the IAST is `sthāpanake` in both), and cuts row 11 short, `बिदा मांगनेकी प्रार्थ` for the heading's `प्रार्थना`. The IAST of the title on p. 19, `ādhyātmika tathā ādhidaivika rūpe sarvarūpatā`, drops the *se* of रूपसे, in the contents and in the heading.
- The paragraph "If divinity were evident, one might believe that He manifests in this way at times …" is printed twice (pp. 36–37).
- The same names and references are written differently: Nalakūbara and Nalakūbera, Nārada and Narada, Śrīkṛṣṇa and Śrikṛṣṇa, Mahāyogī and Mahayogī; `(Bhag. Pura.11.19.3)` beside `(bhāga ।purā ।11 ।19 ।3)`, `(śvetā.upa. 318)` and `( kenopa. 213 )` for 3.8 and 2.3.
- `Élan, a term from which the word allopathy is derived` (p. 5) states an etymology that is not right; facts outside the commentary need checking like everything else.
- The PDF's text layer keeps the English and IAST but scrambles the Devanagari (duplicated vowel signs, private-use characters), so the Devanagari cannot be searched or copied from the PDF. Export with fonts whose Devanagari maps to Unicode (or keep the Word or markdown source with the PDF), so that later editions and readers can work from the text.
