# Preface

When I first encountered the Nalakūbaramaṇigrīvakṛtastuti in 2013 at university, I was deeply moved by its profound humility and dedication. However, I struggled to understand the verse where they sought permission to leave Śrīkṛṣṇa. Despite listening to Goswāmy Śyāma Manohara explanations, I couldn’t comprehend why anyone would ask to leave Śrīkṛṣṇa.

It was only after repeating the course that I began to grasp that the verses must be illustrating the true bhakti that Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva had for Kṛṣṇa—a devotion difficult to comprehend without reaching that spiritual stage.

Some years later, upon seeking advice from Āpaśrī’ on which text to translate, he recommended the Nalakūbaramaṇigrīvakṛtastuti. Initially, I thought that even though I didn’t fully understand the sentiments behind the stuti, it would be a straightforward task given its ten verses. However, I soon realized my mistake. As Śrīmahāprabhujī describes, these verses are as vital as life forces themselves, filled with profound depth and technical terms that were challenging to grasp.

To truly understand its meaning, I immersed myself
<!-- p.ii pdf 3 cont -->
in Āpaśrī’s lectures, reflecting deeply on each verse. There were moments of intense frustration and doubt, where I questioned my ability to do justice to such a sacred text. But recalling Āpaśrī’s vacanāmṛta, which emphasize that all is accomplished by God’s grace, I persevered. The deeper I delved, the more I felt that each verse illustrated the hidden love and respect Nalakūbaramaṇigrīva had for Bhagavān beneath the complex surface.

The translation process was greatly enhanced by the invaluable contributions of Śrīmatībelā Śāha and Śrīmatījalpanā Desāī. Their steadfast support and enthusiastic assistance in editing, verifying the verses, and ensuring the accuracy of translations were crucial, providing me with the encouragement and motivation to continue. I extend my heartfelt thanks to Śrīyogendra Rāyaṭhaṭhṭhā and Śrīmatīnītā Rāyaṭhaṭhṭhā for their unwavering dedication in finalizing and formatting this document. Their efforts made the entire process much smoother and more manageable.

Ultimately, this translation was made possible only through Śrīmahāprabhujī grace and Āpaśrī’s teachings. I feel deeply humbled, recognizing that I do not have the capacity to fully grasp the profound beauty of this devotional stuti.

Śrīmahāprabhujī regards these ten stanzas as essential “Élan,” a term from which the word allopathy is derived. “Élan vital” resonates with the saṃskṛta concept of “prāṇa,” the vital life force. Much like prāṇa transforms us into prāṇī, these ten stanzas represent ten vital elements within the realm of restraint (nirodha). They articulate the essence of how one should practise constraint, the approach to adopt, and how one should seek the guardianship of God. Philosophically, the three forms of God are portrayed: the primordial, causal, and resultant. This serves as an introduction to the Nalakūbara Maṇigrīva stuti.

## Synopsis:

Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva expressed profound praise (stuti) for Bhagavān through ten stanzas, each encapsulating the vital essence—the élan vital. The term ‘prāṇa’ is explicitly enumerated as ten. Within this context, a decisive perspective on knowledge (jñāna) and detachment (vairāgya) is provided. ॥ 1 ॥

The initial introduction presents Bhagavān in His primordial form (mūlarūpa), followed by His manifestation as the universe (jagadrūpa). Similarly, His middle form (madhyarūpa) is detailed, showcasing how Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva depict Bhagavān as the embodiment of knowledge
<!-- p.6 pdf 10 cont -->
in three distinct ways. ॥ 2 ॥

This verse emphasises the difficulty of comprehending Bhagavān completely even after acquiring knowledge in three ways. The greatness (māhātmya) of Bhagavān is described to illustrate His incomprehensibility (durjñeya). Despite taking all forms in this world and being experienced in various manifestations, Bhagavān Himself remains beyond direct experience. ॥ 3 ॥

This transcendence is not attainable merely by seeing Bhagavān as a spiritual (ādhyātmika) or material (ādhibhautika) form. It is only achievable through recognizing Bhagavān as a divine (ādhidaivika) form  (upapanna). From such a divine form of Bhagavān, both spiritual and material forms are manifested. ॥ 4 ॥

Hence, God’s existence as the embracer of all forms and the doer of all becomes evident. This justification ultimately results in the reward of knowledge and devotion. For this reason, after initially elaborating on knowledge, the highest form of devotion is expounded upon towards the conclusion॥ 5॥

Eventually, detachment is achieved through knowledge and devotion. If detachment from worldly matters does not arise from the fruitful knowledge of recognizing their inherent unity with God, and if one neglects to embrace the renunciation of worldly matters, then there will be complete
<!-- p.7 pdf 11 cont -->
destruction of devotion. ॥ 6॥

Thus, the sixth verse expounds on the essential knowledge crucial for achieving devotion. This clarification also aims to dispel any doubt about the possibility of some other emotion that might have inspired Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva to praise God. ॥7॥

Both have humbly bowed to God, seeking His permission to depart, as narrated. They have left God tied to the mortar and are seeking permission—how can this be deemed suitable for any devotee? Hence, the text highlights six qualities of devotion. ॥8॥

Devotion unfolds in the company of fellow devotees, capable of being experienced in the direct presence of the divine or subtly manifesting itself, unrestricted by place. The concealed ‘Rasa’ sentiments of devotion emerge, unveiling the profound richness of personal devotional emotions exclusively in such an environment and in no other way. ॥9॥

In this chapter, the depiction of divine play, or “līlā,” raises the question of whether God intends to illustrate His entwinement with devotees by binding Himself willingly to the mortar to bestow the bliss of devotion and reveal subordination (bhaktavaśyatā), or if He desires to take a central role in the play for His own blissful experience. This is what Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva are not understanding. ॥10॥

The stuti doesn’t distinctly reveal whether God wants
<!-- p.8 pdf 12 cont -->
to personally enjoy the bliss of devotion or generously share its essence with His devotees. Consequently, with no decisive resolution from both perspectives, they were unable to gather the courage to free God from His tender bondage to the mortar. ॥11॥

āgataḥ iti uktam. sā smṛtiḥ sarvalokaprasiddhā bhavatu iti kṛṣṇasvarūpaṃ jñātaṃ nirūpayataḥ, “jñānī priyatamo ato me” (bhāga।purā।11।19।3) ita vākyāt. anyathā sarvaiva stutiḥ virudhyate। tatra prathamaṃ puruṣottamo bhavān iti āhatuḥ

kṛṣṇa ! kṛṣṇa ! iti, ādare vīpsā. kṛṣṇaḥ sadānandaḥ, saeva kṛṣṇanāmā ca ubhayavidhājñānanivṛttyarthaṃ vā tathā uktam. ākṛtyā ceṣṭayā ca na āvayoḥ bhramaḥ iti āhatuḥ mahāyogin iti. laukikāapi nānāyogacaryāyāṃ pravṛttāḥ hīnabhāvaṃ na prāpnuvanti, kutaḥ punaḥ nirdoṣapūrṇaguṇavigrahaḥ ? ato nāmarūpe varṇanīye arthe na bādhake. ādyaḥ iti, mūlabhūtatvameva mahattvaṃ, sarvaiḥ hi svāpekṣayā mahattvaṃ jñātavyam ādyastu tathā. ādyatvaṃ matāntare acetanasyāpi sambhavatīti tadvyāvṛttyartham āha puruṣaḥ iti. sāṃkhyatulyatām āśaṃkya āhatuḥ paraḥ iti puruṣottamaḥ iti arthaḥ. nirākārapakṣanivṛttyarthaṃ ‘puruṣa’ padaṃ, tasmin pakṣe ayaṃ vikṛto bhavedeva. paraḥ kālādīnāmapi niyantā.

evaṃ bhagavato mūlarūpatvaṃ nirūpya kāryarūpābhāve mūlarūpatvaṃ na upapadyataiti kāryasya ca anyathātve tasya gauṇatvam avikṛtatvam asaṃgitvaṃ ca virudhyataiti kāryarūpamapi tvameva iti āhtuḥ vyaktāvyaktam idam iti. idaṃ sarvameva jagad dvirūpameva bhavati. kālena aparigṛhītam avyaktaṃ bhavati. parigṛhītaṃ vyaktaṃ bhavati. ākāśaparamāṇvādīnāmapi vyaktatā iti kecit. tadā sarvameva jagat kālāditṛṇastambāntaṃ vyaktam avyaktaṃ ca bhavati avayutyā anuvādo vā. ubhayathāpi idaṃ jagat tavaiva rūpam. atra pramāṇam āhatuḥ brahmaṇo viduḥ iti,brahmaṇo

vedāda, brāhmaṇāḥ iti vā. te brahmaṇaḥ iti vā. tadā sarvāeva śrutayaḥ pramāṇam iti uktaṃ bhavati. rūpam iti svarūpaṃ nirūpakaṃ vā.

Translation: If there is any doubt about whether these yamalārjuna trees could truly retain memories from their previous birth, then Bhāgavata states that God Himself appeared to clarify this. Without the recollection of such prior births, how could the cursed souls, (trapped in the form of trees, recognize this form of  Śrikṛṣṇa? To make their pre-existing memories known to all, (they described the form of Śrikṛṣṇa as they understood) it is described how they came to recognize the form of Śrikṛṣṇa, as per the statement, “jñānī priyatamo ato me” (Bhag. Pura.11.19.3)  the Lord Himself has acknowledged it.

If the true essence of narrative is not accepted, this entire stuti (praise) will become contradictory. Within this stuti, Bhagavān is initially acknowledged as Puruṣottama. The repetition of ‘Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!’ in this address signifies a profound reverence towards Śrikṛṣṇa even before any other expression. The first instance of ‘Kṛṣṇa’ is synonymous with ‘sad+ānanda,’ and this eternal bliss is also referred to as Kṛṣṇa. Here, ‘Kṛṣ’ signifies ‘Sat,’ and ‘ṇa’ denotes ‘ānanda.’ Therefore, ‘Kṛṣṇa!’ essentially means ‘sadānanda.’ Consequently, both by attribute and name, He is rightfully addressed as Kṛṣṇa.

To dispel ignorance surrounding ātmā and Paramātmā,
<!-- p.12 pdf 16 cont -->
the name ‘Kṛṣṇa’ is repeated twice. Describing Kṛṣṇa as the ‘mahāyogī’ signifies that they were not deceived by Śrikṛṣṇa’s manifestation as a worldly child, engaging in childlike actions, thus recognizing His true nature. In the pursuits of worldly individuals practising yoga caryā, where one takes on two forms and performs accordingly, their actions display no signs of inferiority. Given this, why would one entertain the notion of inadequacy in the Supreme, who is flawless and abundantly virtuous?

Hence, the nāma (concept) and rūpa (percept) of Bhagavān do not interfere with the description of the objects that bear them. When the term ‘ādya’ is employed for Bhagavān, it highlights the significance of being the primordial greatness of His worldly concepts, percept’s, and actions, inviting everyone to understand an additional facet of His divinity. However, alternative viewpoints posit that even insentient nature can be considered primal. To dispel this misconception, the term ‘Puruṣa’ is employed for Bhagavān, aiming to rectify the misguided notion associated with this belief.

As a consequence, within the Sāṅkhya philosophy, despite the consideration of numerous individual puruṣas, none is acknowledged as God. Consequently, the term ‘Puruṣottama’ is attributed to Bhagavān, achieved by combining the adjective ‘par’ (great) with ‘puruṣa’ (individual with form), underscoring the superior nature. The use of the term
<!-- p.13 pdf 17 cont -->
‘puruṣa’ is intended to prevent its conception as formless. Hence, the term ‘puruṣa’ is employed, postulating that if the ‘par’ element is formless, instead of remaining unmodified (avikārī), it undergoes modification due to the influence of illusion (māyā).

‘Para’ indicates the establishment of the ultimate controller, whether it’s kāla karma svabhāva prakṛti or the puruṣa. Thus, after revealing the original form of Bhagavān, if the intended action remains undisclosed or unmanifested, then the true essence of anything eludes us. Moreover, when the action is of opposite nature or conflicts with its cause, the cause is relegated to a subordinate role, in which case the cause being untransformed and unrelated is proved contradictory leading to outcomes opposite to its inherent nature.

Hence, the form of action (kāryarūpa) is also a manifestation of God Himself, revealed through addressing “You (tvama)”. ‘These two forms, whether manifest (vyakta) or unmanifest (avyakta), are known within the world,’ it is stated. By uttering ‘this,’ it signifies that the entire universe embodies a dual nature. Over time (kāla), the unperceived remains unmanifested while the perceived becomes manifested. Some even believe that atoms in the sky manifest. Thus, at that juncture, the entirety of the world can be perceived as both manifest and unmanifest.
